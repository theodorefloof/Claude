## B. Source material referenced in the discussion but not supplied

Nothing below has been seen, and nothing in the deck recreates or describes any of it as fact.

1. **The current pitch deck.** The slides referred to by number, and the "steps one to five" that became modules 2 to 5, come from it. This is the single biggest gap: it holds the module names.
2. **The product playbook.** Checked during the call and described as out of date, so it is not a usable source even if supplied.
3. **The AI task list** held by the demonstration lead. Referenced in the call, never sent. Every amber slot on slide 08 is waiting on it.
4. **The demonstration videos.** Referenced. Not supplied, not described, not represented in the deck.
5. **Scenario names, screenshots and clips** for the selection board. Slide 15 is therefore six labelled empty slots.
6. **The DAM librarian slides** from an earlier pitch for another client. Referenced as existing. Slide 11 describes that option in general terms only, and marks it as neither included nor costed.
7. **The client's written brief**, which is where the definitions of assisted, augmented and automated actually live.

## D. How I read the transcript

**Taken as agreed direction:** the narrative spine (modular production drives the efficiency, three routes deliver it, MAKE is the existing technology that makes it possible, MAKE is modular, AI tasks are the working parts, the client's DAM stays the source of truth, separate today from the transition and the future); MAKE already exists and the deck must make that unmissable; roadmap at theme level rather than feature level; one slide covering the whole AI task set in the written submission; a scenario selection slide from which the client picks two; and more explanation rather than less, because the previous round was simplified until the story stopped landing.

**Taken as rejected:** "Maker Studio" as a second product name; the earlier "human plus tech / fully automated" visual in its current form, because it described the production step only and read as a human-versus-machine split; a separate AI task diagram per scenario in the written submission; and the existing product playbook as a source.

**Taken as unresolved thinking, not decisions:** how to present a DAM librarian; whether to iframe the demo tools so they look like one system; and how many slides the section should run to.

**Excluded as unrelated:** other pitches and their outcomes, and the personal conversation. None of it is reflected anywhere in the deck.

**Transcription quality.** Several names come through inconsistently or garbled, including the product name itself, the demonstration lead's name, and the generation and aggregation tools. I have not guessed at any of them. The product is called MAKE throughout, people are referred to by role, and tools are referred to by category until C6 is settled.

## E. What is in `dist/`

| File | What it is for |
|---|---|
| `technology-storyboard.html` | The deck as 15 fixed pages. Open in a browser, scroll or print. |
| `technology-storyboard.pdf` | The same 15 pages, vector. Best file for reviewing and for sending to a designer. |
| `technology-storyboard.pptx` | 16:9 PowerPoint, one page per slide. Each page carries the vector artwork, so in PowerPoint you can select a page and use Graphics Format > Convert to Shape to break it into editable shapes and text. |
| `svg/slide-01.svg` … `slide-15.svg` | One editable vector file per page, for Illustrator, Figma or PowerPoint. Real text, real shapes, no images. |
| `png/slide-01.png` … | 3200 x 1800 raster, for pasting into email or chat. |

Styling is deliberately rough: white, black, hairlines, and three functional colours (amber for anything open, blue for anything the client owns, grey for roadmap). The thinking and the composition are the deliverable. The finish is not.
