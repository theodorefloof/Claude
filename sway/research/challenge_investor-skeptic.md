# Investor-skeptic challenge: would a seed investor who has buried "trust graph" pitches fund Sway?

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Sway founders' workshop in Barcelona (Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026).

Written from the seat of a seed investor who has passed on, or lost money in, more than one "trust graph", "social commerce" and "affiliate for everyone" company, and who holds positions in creator-commerce businesses today. That last point matters: I am not neutral about ShopMy, LTK, Linktree or Duel. I think they are good at what they do, I think they will notice anything that works, and I read every consumer-first plan through the question "what happens the quarter after this starts working?"

The file does one job: name the eight objections that would make me pass today, say what would change my mind, and offer framings I would find easier to fund. It is deliberately hard on the package and deliberately fair to the incumbents. Nothing here assumes Duel, ShopMy or LTK is weak.

## The short version

I would pass today, and I would say why in the meeting so the founders could come back.

The thesis is good. "Sway turns trusted product recommendations into measurable commerce" is a sentence I would remember, and the founders have already done the hard editorial work of narrowing an expansive vision to a seven-element MVP and a one-city pilot. The parts of the package that remove effort (no follower gate, no app download for the recipient, real products with a personal reason, honest pending/confirmed/reversed labels, one identity, one pass) are the parts I like.

The reasons I would pass are not about the idea. They are that (1) the approved MVP is, feature for feature, something ShopMy, LTK, Linktree or Duel could ship in a quarter, and two of them shipped the consumer half on 30 July 2025; (2) the plan leaves the sales founder with nothing to sell for six to nine months and funds the bridge with rewards that have no budget line; (3) EARN sits on affiliate rails that pay one to five per cent on the products run clubs actually talk about, reverse during validation, and attribute by last click, so "monetise from day one" evaporates in a spreadsheet; (4) the attribution chain breaks at "recipient" unless a stranger gives an email, which means the honest 90-day report is scans and clicks; (5) the moat is a slide, and Duel already publicly claims an "Advocate Intelligence Graph"; (6) the wedge is unsized, leaks to headphones and luggage, and has the worst rails in retail; (7) the team slide reads as a conflict of interest plus a hiring plan; and (8) "a million times better than Duel" drags the company back into the one category where it cannot win.

What would make me lean in is cheap and specific, and it fits inside 90 days without a cheque: one dense node where recipients turn into members without being asked; fifty Lockers whose products cluster around a small number of wellness brands; a recipient page that gets strangers to identify themselves at a rate a brand would pay to see; five brand marketers on the record about which event they would pay for; a coverage spreadsheet that replaces the 10 per cent illustrative rate with real ones; a clean legal position on Blake's contract; and one founder full time by a date. Section 3 sets that out as a table.

I would also fund a different framing of the same insight more readily than the current one. Three are in section 4: Sway as the attribution layer for real-world word of mouth that any platform can plug into; Sway as a consumer-owned recommendation identity with money switched off; and a narrow run-store wedge where the first customer already exists and can see the purchase at the till.

## 1. The eight strongest objections

Ordered by how much of the investment case falls if the objection holds.

### Objection 1. Everything in the MVP is a feature an incumbent could ship in a quarter, and two of them already shipped the consumer half

**The objection in one sentence.** The approved MVP (a five-product list with reasons, a public page, a shareable link, a QR of that link, and click counting) is a product cycle's work for ShopMy, LTK, Linktree, Beacons or Duel, and ShopMy and LTK each launched the consumer-curates-and-shares half of it on 30 July 2025.

**Why it matters.** A pre-seed cheque prices one risk above all others: that the wedge is neutralised before the network exists. The relevant public facts, all at snippet grade:

- ShopMy launched Circles and Trusted By on 30 July 2025; Trusted By is a per-creator count "based on how many shoppers have added you to a Circle, favorited a Circle you're in, or purchased from your links" (https://guide.shopmy.us/circles-wishlist-latest-finds-and-more/5jLY2jskGAENwEWJEf147B/faqs/5kdVRoGw9nmmppW3uMKyRZ ; https://shopmy.us/blog/post/introducing-circles-discovery-reimagined [SNIPPET]). ShopMy reported 175,000+ creators and 1,000+ brand partners in July 2025 (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/shopmy-launches-shopping-platform-a-personalized-destination-built-by-the-creators-you-trust-302516804.html [SNIPPET]).
- LTK gave consumers public profiles with a unique URL and "Lists" of products on 30 July 2025 (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250730281472/en/LTK-Announces-New-LTK-App-Features-Expanding-Its-Role-as-a-Social-Platform-for-Creator-Led-Communities [SNIPPET]).
- Linktree Shops lets a US creator add affiliate products from Linktree's network without joining each programme (https://linktr.ee/features/shops ; https://linktr.ee/blog/affiliate-marketing-linktree-shops [SNIPPET]). Beacons claims an affiliate marketplace of 12,000+ brands (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/beacons-revolutionizes-creator-brand-partnerships-with-beacons-for-brands-and-affiliate-marketplace-302423814.html [SNIPPET]).
- Duel's advocate portal gives advocates "their own shoppable, personalized storefront that showcases their favorite products" and "personalized affiliate and referral links" (https://www.duel.tech/how-it-works/advocate-portal [SNIPPET]); Storefronts 2.0 shipped 13 October 2025 (https://www.duel.tech/blog/drive-your-best-purchase-conversion-with-storefronts-2.0 [SNIPPET]).
- impact.com made creator Storefronts generally available on 11 June 2026 and added customer referrals with QR codes on 30 July 2024 (https://impact.com/press-releases/impact-unveils-ai-creator-commerce-ipx/ ; https://impact.com/press-releases/impact-com-unveils-advocate-referral-solution/ [SNIPPET]).

The founders' defence is that these platforms gate by creator status (ShopMy search visibility requires Trendsetter or Icon status or Trusted By above 100 [SNIPPET, same guide URL]; LTK requires a public profile and an engaged following, https://company.shopltk.com/en-gb/faqs [SNIPPET]) and that Duel requires the brand to sign first. All true. All business choices, not technical barriers. A business choice reverses in one product cycle the moment the person-to-person layer starts to matter, and each of these companies has more distribution today than Sway will have in three years. The pattern I have watched more than once: a startup finds a gap an incumbent left open on purpose, proves there is demand in it, and the incumbent ships the feature to its installed base. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

The competitive PDF concedes the point in its own words: "Sway cannot differentiate on the word trust alone" (`01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt`). The current strategy then describes the opportunity as "the missing connective layer" (`01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`). A layer is what incumbents add.

**What in the documents triggered it.** The MVP durable core in `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` ("account → My 5 → Locker → public profile/recommendation → Sway Link → QR/Sway Pass → basic interaction tracking"); the Linktree and Beacons rows in `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 1; the note in `research/challenge_consumer-behaviour.md` Alternative A that the card-based version "sits closer to a commodity".

**What evidence would settle it.** Three things together. (a) The first cohort is made up mostly of people those platforms would reject or would not want (no public following, no wish to post), who nonetheless produce identified-recipient events; (b) a written, tested reason why an incumbent's own economics punish it for opening to non-creators (for example, that ShopMy's brand proposition is a curated tastemaker network and LTK's is creator content, so an "anyone" tier dilutes what they sell [INFERENCE]); (c) recipient-side data (a named recipient, an in-person context, a reason) that a cookie model cannot produce and that at least one brand marketer says it values.

**Cheapest test in the next four weeks or at the workshop.** Before Thursday 8 October, Blake builds the same "My 5" in an LTK consumer profile and in Linktree or Beacons, thirty minutes each, and screenshots both. At the workshop, put them beside the Sway prototype in front of three to five community members (or, failing that, each founder plays a hostile product manager) and ask: "What does the Sway one let you do that these do not?" If the honest answer is "it looks nicer" or "it has a QR", the wedge is a feature. Second exercise, twenty minutes: each founder writes the one-paragraph memo an LTK product manager would send to kill Sway, and then the reply. The reply is the differentiation thesis, or there is not one.

### Objection 2. The cold start is three-sided, the sales founder has nothing to sell for months, and the bridge has no budget

**The objection in one sentence.** The plan is consumer first, brand later, affiliate in between, which means that for the first six to nine months the sales lead has no product to sell, the product lead has no engineer, and the only bridge across the gap (Sway-funded Founding Member rewards) has no budget line anywhere in the package.

**Why it matters.** Trust-graph companies die in the gap between "useful to one person" and "dense enough to interest a buyer". The founders have rightly said the product must be valuable before a brand pays (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`, non-negotiable 3). But look at what the plan asks:

- Member numbers of 250 to 500 by day 60 and 750 to 1,500 by day 90 (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` [ILLUSTRATIVE]). Paid media is ruled out ("Do not target 'wellness consumers' broadly with paid media"). So acquisition is founder-led outreach at run clubs and studios. Even the low end of the day-90 number is roughly eight new members per day, every day, for ninety days, by two people with day jobs, while also recruiting the nodes, running rewards and iterating the product. [INFERENCE from the PDF's own numbers]
- The only organic channel is recipient-to-member conversion, which cannot be tested before a product exists (`research/context_audit.md` 2.5) and is structurally weak because "the recipient's job is finished before Sway asks anything of them" (`research/challenge_consumer-behaviour.md` objection 6).
- The bridge is "Sway funds lightweight, culturally relevant ways to recognize and reward participation" (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`): classes, recovery sessions, race entries, product drops. `research/context_audit.md` 6.2 item 6: "No figure exists anywhere in the package." An investor who is told rewards are the retention mechanism and is then told there is no rewards budget concludes the retention mechanism does not exist.
- EARN, the other bridge, is gated by a fourth party: affiliate networks' approval (Amazon Associates closes applications without three qualifying sales within 180 days; Awin, impact.com and Rakuten need network sign-up then per-advertiser approval, days to weeks, `research/prototype_feasibility.md` 4.2 [SNIPPET for Amazon; timing UNVERIFIED for the networks]).

So this is not a two-sided cold start. It is advocates, recipients and brands, with a fourth gate on the money. And the pitch is an orphan: a consumer investor is asked to underwrite a B2B exit, and a B2B investor is asked to underwrite consumer behaviour (`research/investor_lens.md` 1.2). Meanwhile Tory, the commercial founder and idea originator, spends the period as a community recruiter. That is a fine job, but it is not the job the "why us" slide describes.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` launch sequence ("Start with consumers, not brand contracts"); the 90-day table in `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`; `05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md` "What member benefit is strong enough before brands arrive?"; `research/context_audit.md` 5.1 (the "middle path" of unpaid brand observers) and 6.2 (no budget, no hours).

**What evidence would settle it.** One node reaching a penetration where recommendations land on other members, with the leader's active support (the consumer-behaviour file suggests 30 per cent of active members within 60 days [PRACTITIONER VIEW]); unprompted joins from recipients; a rewards budget with unit costs from real venues and a cap; two wellness brands agreeing in writing to be unpaid observers of anonymised pilot data with a right of first refusal on the first Collective; and a calendar showing Tory holding brand and community conversations through the pilot even without a product.

**Cheapest test.** Before Thursday 8 October: Tory gets a written "yes, I will host a session" from one community leader in the candidate city and an email from one wellness brand marketer agreeing to look at pilot data; Blake prices five candidate rewards with two venues and asks whether they would gift or co-fund. At the workshop: write the day-90 member number the founders actually believe, divide it by working days and by founder evenings available; if it needs more than one founder evening per node per week, cut the number, not the hours. Then write the single sentence that replaces the brand as the reason a member keeps going (`research/challenge_consumer-behaviour.md` prompt 14) and say whether either founder believes it.

### Objection 3. EARN sits on rails Sway does not control, that pay cents in wellness, that reverse, and that attribute by last click

**The objection in one sentence.** The revenue model's worked examples use a 10 per cent "standard rate" and a 16 to 20 per cent "Collective target", but the public rails in the launch category pay 1 to 5 per cent on the products run clubs actually talk about, reverse commissions during validation, close accounts for low volume, and let a coupon extension overwrite the recommender's cookie at checkout.

**Why it matters.** Three separate ways.

First, the deck's investor point "Sway can monetize before a brand becomes a direct customer" (`04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt` p6) will be run in a spreadsheet during the meeting. The inputs an investor will use: cohort size × recommendations per member per month × click-through × conversion × basket × rate × Sway's 20 per cent. On public rates the rate term is small: HOKA's own affiliate page is reported at 5 per cent on full-price items and 3 per cent on sale (https://www.hoka.com/en/us/hoka-affiliate-program.html [SNIPPET; a directory claims up to 14 per cent, THIRD-PARTY and conflicting]); Amazon Associates Health and Personal Care is reported at about 1 per cent (https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/ [THIRD-PARTY; official schedule blocked]); lululemon about 5 to 10 per cent (https://www.creator-hero.com/blog/lululemon-affiliate-program-in-depth-review-pros-and-cons [THIRD-PARTY]); direct supplement programmes 8 to 12 per cent (https://getlasso.co/niche/supplements/ [THIRD-PARTY]). A $100 HOKA purchase at 5 per cent is $5 gross, $4 to the advocate and $1 to Sway. Whatever the cohort does, Sway's share is immaterial at pilot scale, and an investor who hears "day-one revenue" will stop listening (`research/investor_lens.md` 1.4 says the same).

Second, the advocate's first experience of money will be "pending", then "reversed", then a few pence. Awin pays "validated" commissions (https://www.awin.com/gb/how-to-use-awin/introduction-to-publisher-payments [SNIPPET]); the revenue PDF itself notes that "Affiliate networks commonly allow actions to be modified or reversed during a locking period for returns, cancellations or other contract conditions". Footwear and apparel are high-return categories [NEEDS SOURCE; nrf.com blocked, no figure captured]. The guardrail "Do not make the experience feel like couponing" is right, and a reversed sixty-pence commission is worse than couponing: it is couponing that did not pay.

Third, and this is the one I would press hardest: Sway becomes a sub-affiliate. "Purchase, where visible" means the network's last-click cookie survived the 7 to 30 day window (LTK cookie windows commonly 7 to 14 days, https://instantdm.com/blog/ltk-affiliate-program [THIRD-PARTY]) against coupon sites and browser extensions. The Honey case showed an extension "swapping the creator's affiliate tracking for its own at checkout" (https://digiday.com/marketing/the-honey-scandal-is-a-wake-up-call-for-the-creator-industrys-affiliate-partnerships/ [SNIPPET]; primary coverage at theverge.com could not be fetched). The survey-versus-pixel gap the founders cite against brands ("2% in Platform Data versus 23% in Survey Data", https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide [SNIPPET, vendor]) applies with full force to Sway's own purchase data. Sway's honesty about attribution then means showing brands the pixel number, not the survey number, and the pixel number is the one brands already have.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt` pages 1 and 3 (the 10, 16, 18 and 20 per cent examples); EARN as step five of the spine; the Trust "Affiliate" view listing "commission, conversion rate, and pending/confirmed/reversed status" (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`); "Eligible recommendations generate affiliate revenue from day one" (`02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` p4).

**What evidence would settle it.** A coverage spreadsheet of the actual products named by ten My 5 respondents: network, programme, rate, cookie window, sub-ID support, approval requirements, days to approval, plus the share of products with no programme at all. A model of one $100 sale at a real rate through one real network with the network's fee treatment stated (Awin describes a network fee but publishes no rate, https://www.awin.com/us/faqs [SNIPPET]). And a founder decision on whether EARN is in the Alpha at all or is a later layer with money switched off.

**Cheapest test.** Two hours for the spreadsheet (`research/context_audit.md` 2.14). This week, Tory applies to Awin and impact.com as a publisher to measure approval times and to read the actual locking-period and sub-ID terms, which no sibling file could fetch. Blake removes every currency amount from the prototype and uses the label the prototype file recommends: "Earnings: 1 pending (affiliate eligibility not yet confirmed)". At the workshop, decide whether the pilot runs with money off, and if so, what the spine word EARN means for the first cohort.

### Objection 4. The attribution chain breaks at "recipient" and at "purchase", which leaves Sway reporting the same events as every link tool, and the honesty guardrail then works against the pitch

**The objection in one sentence.** Person-to-person attribution needs the recipient to identify themselves (email save or sign-up) and the purchase to come back from a network with the sub-ID intact; without both, Sway's honest 90-day report is scans, views and outbound clicks over a sparse city-level map, and a CFO who waves away Duel's earned media value will wave away "scans" faster.

**Why it matters.** The differentiation from composite metrics is "observable events a finance person can audit" (`research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 7, implication 1). I agree with the principle. But observable events with no identified recipient are not a graph; they are a clickstream with a QR in front. Specifically:

- The recipient page asks the recipient to "optionally save the product using email" and "optionally join/download Sway" (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`). Both optional, both after the recipient has already got what they came for. The identification rate is unknown and, on my experience of referral flows, low [PRACTITIONER VIEW; NEEDS SOURCE for any benchmark].
- The "Real World" Trust view is "an actual geographic map using approximate city-level influence" (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`). Two dots on a map imply an edge between two people; the prototype will draw that edge from a scan, not from an identified person. The design says "Preserve unknowns" and "Do not infer a purchase from a click or a trusted relationship from raw activity alone", which is right, and it means the map must show much less than it will look like it shows.
- The events are fakeable: two housemates scanning each other (`research/challenge_consumer-behaviour.md` prompt 6), a member scanning her own Pass from a second phone, the same recipient scanning twice. "Trust should only strengthen when behavior provides evidence that other people value someone's recommendation" (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`) is the right rule, and the package does not yet say which event counts as that evidence.
- The recipient is a data subject who has not joined. A persistent record of "who recommended what to whom, where" is personal data about the recipient as well as the advocate; the UK GDPR principles of purpose limitation and data minimisation are the ones at stake (`research/investor_lens.md` 4.4 [UNVERIFIED wording, pages blocked]). The more identified the recipient, the better the graph and the heavier the obligation.

The contrast the founders want to draw with Duel's Gross Advocacy Value (a composite of "tracked affiliate or conversion data" and "earned media value", https://www.duel.tech/glossary/gross-advocacy-value [SNIPPET]) only works if Sway's events are both honest and material. Honest and immaterial loses to composite and large in most marketing budget meetings. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

**What in the documents triggered it.** The attribution chain "Advocate → product → recommendation → recipient → action → purchase, where visible" and the recipient progression in `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`; `research/context_audit.md` assumption 8 and hypotheses 2.17 and 2.18; the geolocation mock in `research/prototype_feasibility.md` section 6.

**What evidence would settle it.** An attribution event table with, for each event: what is known, what is inferred, who can fake it and how, what the advocate sees, what a brand would see. A projection of event volume for 100 members over 90 days at stated rates. The recipient identification rate from the first cohort. One brand marketer choosing the row they would pay to see, in their words.

**Cheapest test.** At the workshop, run Day 2 item 3 ("Define attribution events and what Sway can and cannot truthfully claim") as a hostile audit: for each row, what would a CFO say? Then thirty minutes of "how would we cheat this?" on the prototype, with the fixes written down. Before the workshop: put a real recipient page (any landing-page tool, a real product, a real reason, a real "continue" link) in front of five people and count how many give an email to save the product. If it is zero of five, the recipient page needs a different reason to identify, and the deck cannot describe a graph.

### Objection 5. The moat is asserted, not designed, and the incumbents already own the vocabulary

**The objection in one sentence.** "A growing graph of human influence" is a slide, not an asset: Duel publicly claims a "proprietary Advocate Intelligence Graph", ShopMy publishes a Trusted By score, impact.com and Awin hold the conversion data, and a graph is a moat only when it has volume, exclusivity and a decision that changes because of it, none of which Sway can have in the wedge phase.

**Why it matters.** I have watched "we will own the graph" pitches in social commerce and referral networks reach a few thousand users, never the density at which anyone would pay for the graph, and die as tools with a small base. [PRACTITIONER VIEW; I could not verify a named list of such companies in this session, NEEDS SOURCE.] The lesson I took: a data moat compounds only after the product wins on its own; it is never the reason the product wins. The package inverts this. `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`: "The defensible asset is a growing graph of human influence... The QR code, affiliate link, and wallet pass are mechanisms; the persistent history of trusted influence is the strategic asset." `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` p3: "THE ASSET COMPOUNDS".

Three problems with that as an investor story:

- The words are taken. Duel's G2 listing: "With our proprietary Advocate Intelligence Graph that understands every individual" (https://www.g2.com/products/duel/reviews [SNIPPET, vendor-supplied text; what it actually is, NEEDS SOURCE]). Duel's press release: "the single system of record for every type of advocate" (https://pulse2.com/duel-16-million-raised-to-transform-brand-advocacy/ [press release via SNIPPET]). ShopMy's Trusted By is a published trust number. A pitch that says "our moat is the graph" is a pitch the audience has heard from the funded incumbent.
- The competitive PDF's own definition of the moat is five things the MVP does not build: "IRL + digital signals, person-to-person propagation, category-specific Trust, community context and persistent behavior over time" (`01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt`). The MVP builds scans and clicks with a city label.
- Exclusivity is limited by law and by the product's own honesty. The most valuable data (who influences whom) is personal data about two people; data minimisation and the recipient's rights cap how much Sway can hold and for how long [UNVERIFIED wording]. And a brand that pays for a Collective will expect to export its advocates' data (brands "strongly value owning the relationship and data", `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 7 [INFERENCE]), which leaks the graph one brand at a time.

There is a real moat candidate hiding in the package, and it is not the graph. It is density in a physical community: if a third of a run club's regulars keep their Lockers in Sway and recommend to each other through it, a competitor has to be physically present in that club to replicate the record. That is a moat by presence, one node at a time, and it is honest. [INFERENCE]

**What in the documents triggered it.** `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` "Long-term moat"; `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` p3; `01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt` p1 and p2; `research/context_audit.md` 2.11 and 5.5.

**What evidence would settle it.** A written answer to: what will Sway hold on day 90 that none of ShopMy, LTK, impact.com or Duel could obtain from their existing data or a post-purchase survey, and which brand decision changes because of it. A density target per node at which replication requires physical presence. One brand marketer saying "that changes my decision on X".

**Cheapest test.** Forty-five minutes at the workshop. Each founder writes the "data we will hold at day 90" list. Then cross out every row an incumbent could get from its own data or from a "how did you hear about us" survey. What remains is the moat candidate. If nothing remains, the deck says "the tool is the wedge; the map is the ambition; the pilot proves the first" (`research/context_audit.md` 5.5) and the word "moat" leaves the deck until a pilot produces one surprising, true pattern.

### Objection 6. Wellness is the launch territory with the weakest rails, a diffuse brand set, leaking Lockers and no sizing

**The objection in one sentence.** The wedge is "measurable word of mouth for wellness brands, built through dense wellness and healthy-living communities" in one city, but wellness rails pay the least, the brand set is spread across supplements, footwear, apparel, recovery, sleep and skincare, My 5 explicitly admits headphones and luggage, and no wellness word-of-mouth or community-size figure exists anywhere in the package, so an investor sees a hobby, not a beachhead.

**Why it matters.** Nobody expects a market-size slide at pre-seed. They expect a legible path from wedge to category. That path needs brand concentration: many members recommending the same brand in the same city, so that "Your community already exists. We found it." (`04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`) has a named brand on the other end of the call. If the first 100 Lockers hold 300 distinct products across 200 brands, there is nobody to call. ## 2. What must be true for Sway to work, from this lens

Each is written so it can be false. If the founders cannot say how they would know, it belongs on the evidence-gap slide, not the thesis slide.

1. **A material share of recipients identify themselves.** Without an email save or a join at the recipient end, there is no person-to-person edge, only a click with a QR in front of it, and everything downstream (Trust, the map, the brand pitch) is a link tool's report. The founders set the threshold; I would want it stated before the pilot and measured from the first week. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

2. **The first fifty Lockers concentrate around a small number of wellness brands in one city.** The brand pitch is "we found your community". That requires at least one brand with five or more recommenders in the same node, and a large enough share of wellness-brand products that a wellness marketer sees themselves in the data. If Lockers are mostly headphones, luggage and Amazon supplements, Phase 4 has no one to call.

3. **Members return on the core action with money switched off.** On public wellness rates, earnings are immaterial for years. If the only members who return are those who earned, Sway is an affiliate utility for people who could already use one, and the "everyone can have influence" positioning has no user behind it.

4. **The recipient page outperforms a pasted brand link on action rate.** If a WhatsApp message with the retailer URL gets the same outcome, Sway is a slower WhatsApp with a disclosure line. This is testable with a landing page and five people before the workshop.

5. **Sway's own first-party events stay valuable when the affiliate network's last-click data disagrees.** If a brand only believes the network's number, Sway's honest events are decoration on someone else's attribution. The value has to be in what the network cannot see (identified recipient, in-person context, reason, repeat), and a brand has to say so.

6. **One founder is full time by a stated date, the legal position on Blake's contract and on IP in the existing materials is clean and written down, and a technical partner is named.** Nothing in the plan survives two part-timers with an unresolved conflict and no engineer.

7. **Density in one physical node is reachable by hand and is itself the defensible asset.** A third of a club's regulars keeping and using Lockers in Sway is something a competitor must be physically present to replicate; a scattering of Lockers across a city is not. The founders can be at that node weekly for twelve weeks.

## 4. Stronger alternatives and reframings

Each keeps the founders' insight (recommendation between people is the most trusted input to a purchase, mostly invisible, and the people who do it are mostly not creators) and changes the part that makes the current framing hard to fund. Each has its own biggest risk. I would fund A or C more readily than the current framing; B is the purest version of the idea and the hardest to fund.

### Alternative A. The attribution layer for real-world word of mouth that any platform can plug into

**The alternative.** Sway stops being a consumer network with a brand business behind it and becomes the standard for one event: an identified, in-person or dark-social recommendation from a named person to a named recipient, with a product and a reason. The Sway Pass and Sway Link are the capture mechanism; the recipient page is the product; the event goes wherever the brand already keeps its attribution (its referral platform, its affiliate network sub-ID, its CRM). Sway sells the event, not the community.

**What it changes.** Product: the recipient page, the event schema, consent and disclosure, and an export or webhook become the whole product; the Locker shrinks to "the products I have recommended", the Trust map becomes a report, Points and Collectives disappear. Sales: Tory sells now, to two kinds of buyer who exist today: brands that already run a referral or ambassador programme and cannot see in-person recommendation, and the platforms those programmes run on. The pitch is "plug in the recommendations that happen at the run club" and the first customer could be a wellness brand with an existing Mention Me, Yotpo or impact.com programme. Investor story: infrastructure, business-to-business-to-consumer, priced per identified event or per seat; the comparables are post-purchase attribution tools such as Fairing (https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide [SNIPPET, vendor]) and referral platforms, not ShopMy. The category-wide pain it addresses is the one every review in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` points at: "link and code tracking shows only the smallest portion of influencer impact" and proving value to finance.

**What it keeps.** The attribution chain and the honesty rule ("Preserve unknowns"); the Sway Pass as "the real-world counterpart of an affiliate link"; the recipient journey exactly as designed; wellness as the first vertical; the two-surface, one-engine architecture in `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`.

**Biggest risk.** It becomes a feature of the platforms it plugs into. Brand-scoped QR and wallet-pass referral already exists (impact.com referral QR, Extole receipt QR, Talkable Wallet, Mention Me Name Share; `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 4 [SNIPPET]) and the one thing those vendors lack, a cross-brand consumer identity, is the thing this framing gives up. There is also no consumer story for Blake to build, and the "everyone can have influence" mission becomes a data pipe. If the founders choose A, they should choose it knowing it is a smaller, faster, more fundable company than the one in the PDFs.

### Alternative B. The consumer-owned recommendation identity, with money switched off

**The alternative.** Sway is the record of what you actually use and stand behind, owned by you, portable, private by default, shared one item or one list at a time. Its promise is "your recommendations are yours, not a platform's feed and not a brand's programme". Earning is off at launch; attribution is on but invisible to the member except as "what happened after I shared this". Brands appear only when the record is large enough to be interesting, and then only as buyers of anonymised patterns.

**What it changes.** Product: the Locker becomes verdicts ("bought it, would buy again", the shape `research/challenge_consumer-behaviour.md` Alternative B proposes) rather than a public list; export and deletion are first-class; the Pass is a way to share one item, not a profile; no Points, no Progress, no map in the first version. Sales: none for a long time, deliberately; Tory's job becomes community distribution and brand discovery conversations without an offer. Investor story: a consumer network judged on retention and on the share of members who share at least one item a month; the comparison set is consumer curation products (The Mall, positioned as "Letterboxd for shopping", https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/a-new-app-the-mall-is-building-a-universal-feed-for-online-shopping/ [SNIPPET]) and the pitch is to consumer investors only.

**What it keeps.** My 5, the Locker as home, one identity, real products with genuine reasons, honest attribution, wellness communities as acquisition nodes, and the Trust Map as an eventual asset built from consented, member-owned records.

**Biggest risk.** Consumer recommendation and social-shopping products have a long record of reaching modest scale and stalling; I could not verify a named list in this session and will not cite one from memory [NEEDS SOURCE], but the pattern is why my first question to any such pitch is "what does one person get on day one with nobody else on it?" Money off removes the one incentive that is easy to explain, retention has to come from the record alone, and the brand revenue moves years out. This is the version of Sway most consistent with the founders' guardrails and the least fundable at pre-seed by anyone who is not a pure consumer investor. It also does not use Tory's skills for a long time.

### Alternative C. The narrow "run store" wedge: the first customer already exists and can see the purchase at the till

**The alternative.** Choose specialty running retail as the first customer. The run store is where run clubs meet, where gait analysis and shoe advice already happen, where the products in a runner's Locker are on the wall, and where the owner already sponsors clubs, hosts group runs and feels the margin. Sway becomes the store's "recommended by a real runner" layer: a club member's Sway Pass or Link resolves to the store's stock; the recipient buys in store with a code or online through the store's site; the store sees the purchase at its own point of sale, with no affiliate network, no cookie window, no last-click hijack and no reversal ambiguity. The store pays per identified purchase or a small monthly fee, and rewards recommenders with things a store can give (fitting priority, event entry, early access to drops).

**What it changes.** Product: the recipient page resolves to a store product and a redemption mechanism; a simple store dashboard (who recommended, who bought, what) replaces the brand Collective; the Locker is seeded from the store's catalogue, which solves the product-image and product-data problem in `research/prototype_feasibility.md` section 5. Sales: Tory sells to store owners now, in person, in the launch city; they are reachable, they have budgets, and a first paying customer is weeks away rather than quarters. Investor story: a vertical software-plus-network business in specialty running and, later, cycling, yoga and climbing retail, with brands (HOKA and its peers) reached through the retailers they already depend on; comparables are vertical retail software with a consumer surface, not creator commerce. The attribution is first-party and complete, which fixes Objections 3 and 4 outright.

**What it keeps.** The IRL Sway Pass as the hero and the reason to exist; honest attribution; no follower gate; the Locker with real products and reasons; wellness communities as acquisition nodes; the long-run ambition of a cross-brand record, built store by store and club by club.

**Biggest risk.** The market is narrow. Specialty run retail in one country is a small number of independent stores and a few chains [NEEDS SOURCE for a count]; margins are thin; a store may see Sway as a referral tool it could get from its point-of-sale vendor, and the first version looks like "Mention Me for shops". It breaks non-negotiable 2 as written ("Sway is not a run-club-only product") and the founders have already decided against it once. The path from stores to brands to a cross-brand consumer network is real but long, and a store-first Sway would have to keep the consumer Locker useful outside the store or it never becomes the company in the PDFs.

### Alternative D. The hybrid I would actually push for: consumer-first with two named design-partner brands as unpaid observers

**The alternative.** Keep the current consumer-first wedge, but do not run it alone. Before the pilot, sign two wellness brands that already sponsor communities in the launch city as design partners: they pay nothing, they see anonymised pilot patterns monthly, they answer the attribution questions, and they hold a right of first refusal on the first Brand Collective. This is the "middle path" already noted in `research/context_audit.md` 5.1, made into a commitment with names.

**What it changes.** Product: almost nothing; a monthly anonymised report is a spreadsheet. Sales: Tory has a job from week one (recruit the two brands, run the monthly conversations, write the first offer from what they say). Investor story: the same consumer network, but with brand pull evidenced on the record from the first month rather than promised for Phase 4, and with a named first customer for the intelligence layer.

**What it keeps.** Everything in the current spine and MVP boundary.

**Biggest risk.** The brands shape the pilot toward themselves (their products, their clubs), which biases the "we found your community" proof, and the brands' presence leaks into the member experience and breaks non-negotiable 3 in spirit. The founders will need a written rule on what the brands see and do not see, and the discipline to report a null result to them.

### A note on choosing

[INFERENCE] A and C are both "attribution first, community second" and both give Tory something to sell now; C is A with a first customer who can see the purchase. B is the founders' instinct taken to its end and belongs to a different kind of investor. D is the cheapest change to the current plan and closes Objections 2 and 8 without reopening scope. Whatever the choice, it should be recorded as a decision with a review trigger, per the brief's design principles, not left as a framing debate.

## 6. The single question I would ask the founders first

"Which one of you is full time on Sway by 1 January 2027, and what does the other one's employment contract say about it?"

I would ask it first because every other question in this file has a workable answer only if this one does. The thesis can be sharpened, the wedge narrowed, the rails replaced, the moat reframed. None of that helps if the company has two part-time founders, one of whom is a senior employee of the incumbent the pitch measures itself against, and no written position on what that employee may build, own and say. An investor who does not ask it first will ask it in reference calls, and by then the answer is worth less.

The second question, for the thesis rather than the team, would be: "Show me one recommendation from last week, from one real person to one named recipient, that Sway would have recorded and that none of ShopMy, LTK, Linktree, WhatsApp or a brand's referral tool would have seen. Then tell me what a brand would have paid to see it." If the founders can answer that with a real example and a real marketer's words, the rest of this file becomes a to-do list rather than a pass.
