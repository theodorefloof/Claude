# Consumer-behaviour challenge: will anyone actually build, keep and use a Sway?

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Barcelona founder workshop (Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026).

Lens: consumer behaviour and growth, with particular attention to why utility apps get abandoned and why sharing mechanics die on contact with real social moments. The question underneath every section: will a wellness-community member build a Locker, keep it current, and say "scan my Sway" out loud to another person, more than once?

## The short version

Sway asks a wellness-community member to adopt four new behaviours: answer a list question (My 5), keep the list current (Locker), present a QR code inside a conversation (Sway Pass), and come back to look at points, a map and earnings. Each replaces a behaviour that already works with no friction at all: say the brand name; say "I'll send you the link" and paste it into WhatsApp later; keep favourites in your head, in Notes or in Instagram saves; receive nothing for the favour and be fine with that (`research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 3 describes this baseline honestly). The package has not yet observed any of the four new behaviours in one real person; `research/context_audit.md` section 2 lists twenty-two hypotheses and no observations.

The strongest parts of the thesis, from this lens, are the ones that remove effort: no follower gate, no app download for the recipient, product image first, honest attribution, one identity and one pass. The weakest parts are the ones that add effort or change social meaning: the scan choreography, the public taste profile, points for actions that can be faked, and money that is visible but tiny. My objections are ordered by how much of the thesis falls if the objection is right. Every one has a test two people can run before Friday 9 October without an engineer.

## 1. The eight strongest objections

### Objection 1. The scan asks the wrong person to do the work, and is slower than the behaviour it replaces

**In one sentence.** "Scan my Sway" requires the recipient to unlock a phone, open a camera, hold it steady, tap a banner and wait for a page, all for a benefit that accrues mostly to the advocate and to Sway, while the incumbent behaviour ("they're HOKA Cliftons", or "I'll send you the link") costs the recipient nothing.

**Why it matters.** The Sway Pass is the only mechanic in the package that ShopMy, LTK, Beacons and Linktree do not already offer (non-negotiable 5: "A Sway Pass is the real-world counterpart of an affiliate link"), and `research/context_audit.md` section 4 already ranks it the most consequential assumption. [PRACTITIONER VIEW] A new sharing mechanic displaces an old one only when it is faster for the person being asked to change, or when it gives that person something the old way cannot. A scan is slower than speech. What it gives the recipient is conditional in the documents' own words: "The recipient benefit, if one genuinely exists" (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`). [INFERENCE] The personal Pass makes it worse, not better: `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md` says "A personal Pass begins a short recommendation session; the advocate selects the product from the Locker, optionally edits the reason", so after the recipient scans, the advocate must also go back to her own phone and pick the product. That is a two-phone, three-step choreography in a moment that today is one person speaking. Add the physical context of the launch nodes (phones in armbands or lockers, sweaty hands, a group moving) and the window for the scan narrows to the coffee afterwards, which is also the moment "just WhatsApp it to me" is easiest.

Brand-side referral products already ship QR and wallet-pass mechanics (Talkable Wallet for ALO Yoga and PUMA; impact.com/advocate QR in widgets and order inserts; Extole QR on receipts; all [SNIPPET] via `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 4), so the mechanic is not novel in itself; what is unclaimed is the person-to-person, cross-brand version, and that is precisely the version with the social friction.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`: "'Where did you get that?' → 'Scan my Sway.'" `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md` advocate journey steps 5 and 6 ("Tori opens View My Sway Pass from the Locker... Blake scans. Tori can select the relevant product immediately"). `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` first proof point 2: "Use it naturally when someone asks what they recommend."

**What evidence would settle it.** A ratio from real moments, not a demo: over two weeks, of every moment in which someone asked an advocate what she uses, in how many did she offer the Pass, in how many did the other person scan, how many seconds did it take from "can I see?" to product page, and how often did the other person say "just send it to me". [PRACTITIONER VIEW] If the Pass is used in fewer than one in five of the moments where it could have been, the Pass is a fallback and the Link is the hero; the audit's tension 5.2 gets resolved by data rather than by preference.

**Cheapest test.** The Wizard of Oz week already proposed in `research/context_audit.md` 2.2 and 2.3, with three additions that turn it into a behavioural measurement: (a) each participant tallies "moments where I could have used it" as well as "moments where I did", because the ratio is the finding; (b) each writes down the first thing the other person said; (c) half the participants carry the QR printed on a card or phone case and half show it on screen, because the on-screen version has an unlock-and-find step that the printed version does not. Five people, one week, start by Monday 21 September, tallies back by Friday 2 October. Owner: whichever founder lives nearest a run club or studio. Both founders should also be the recipient of a scan twice, with someone who is not the other founder. During the workshop: time the prototype scan with a stopwatch from "can I see that?" to product page; if it is over fifteen seconds, the demo script leads with the Link and shows the Pass second.

### Objection 2. My 5 is a one-time artefact, and the Locker has no natural return frequency

**In one sentence.** "Five things worth the money" is a good conversation starter that people will enjoy answering once, after which the Locker changes only as often as a person acquires a new product she would put her name behind, which for most wellness-community members is a handful of times a year.

**Why it matters.** The retention claim in `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` is that people "Return because the Locker, progress, recognition, and rewards are valuable." [PRACTITIONER VIEW] A utility that has no daily or weekly reason to be opened survives only if it is reachable at the moment its external trigger fires. Sway's trigger ("someone asks me") fires outside the app, in a group chat or at a session. If the Locker lives on a home screen, it will be forgotten by the time the trigger fires; if it lives in the share sheet, in Wallet and in the WhatsApp keyboard, it can be reached. `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` calls My 5 "A beautiful, shareable list," and the nearest consumer precedents for beautiful shareable lists (year-in-review cards, top-nine grids) are shared once and never maintained [PRACTITIONER VIEW; NEEDS SOURCE for any comparative figure]. Published consumer-app retention benchmarks could not be fetched in this session; `research/investor_lens.md` 6.2 lists them as [NEEDS SOURCE]. The founders should not put a retention number in the deck without one.

There is also a content problem hiding inside the frequency problem. `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` says "We acquire through wellness; we do not restrict the person's Sway to wellness," and gives an example list of HOKA, Oura, LMNT, Supergoop and Theragun. [INFERENCE] The honest answer to "what is actually worth the money?" from most people includes headphones, a vacuum cleaner, a water bottle, a mattress and a book. Those are bought on Amazon, where the health category reportedly pays about 1 per cent [UNVERIFIED third-party, `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 5], and they are not the wellness brand set Sway wants to show a brand in Phase 4. The Locker that people will happily build is not the Locker the brand story needs.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` MVP item 1 ("After one product, immediately preview the shareable result. Then ask, 'What else is worth the money?'") and "My Locker: A living home for everything they recommend beyond the first five." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` first-proof metrics: "Locker quality and repeat additions."

**What evidence would settle it.** Two numbers from ten to fifteen community members: how many products they have started swearing by in the last three months (additions per quarter), and where they keep their recommendations today (nowhere, head, Notes, Instagram saves, Amazon list, a WhatsApp message to themselves). Plus, from the Wizard of Oz week: how many participants added anything after day one without being prompted.

**Cheapest test.** Fold two questions into the ten My 5 conversations already proposed in `research/context_audit.md` 2.1: "How many things have you started recommending in the last three months, and what were they?" and "Where is your list now?" Then categorise every product named across all respondents into: wellness brand with its own site; wellness product bought on Amazon; non-wellness. Report three percentages by Friday 2 October. In the Wizard of Oz week, do not remind anyone to add products; at day 14 ask "what did you add, and why then?" At the workshop: decide whether the Alpha's home is an app at all, or a web page plus a Wallet pass plus a share-sheet entry, and write the "where does Sway live at the moment someone asks" answer in one line.

### Objection 3. There is no single-player value in months one to six: the advocate gets a list, a link, pennies and a promise

**In one sentence.** Under the plan's own sequencing, for at least the first two phases the advocate receives a list she could keep in Notes, a link she could make in Beacons, affiliate earnings of a few dollars at wellness rates, points redeemable against unbudgeted rewards, and "recognition" from brands that cannot exist until a brand pays.

**Why it matters.** Non-negotiable 3 says "The consumer product must be valuable before a brand becomes a customer," which is the right principle, but the package does not name the value. Take each candidate in turn. Money: HOKA's affiliate programme is reported at 5 per cent on full price and 3 per cent on sale [SNIPPET, https://www.hoka.com/en/us/hoka-affiliate-program.html via `research/alternatives_landscape.md`], so a USD 100 purchase yields USD 4 to the advocate under the 80/20 rule; Amazon health and personal care is reported at about 1 per cent [UNVERIFIED third-party, https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/], so a USD 100 electrolyte order yields under USD 1. Recognition: `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` says "HOKA notices," but under `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` brands are approached only at step 5, after density, so no brand can notice anyone for months. Rewards: `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` lists "classes, recovery sessions, race entries, product drops," and `research/context_audit.md` 6.2 confirms no budget exists anywhere in the package. Discovery: "what do the people I trust swear by?" is explicitly Phase 2. So the day-one value is the list and the link.

Here Duel's public model is the stronger design for motivation, and it is fair to say so: on the public "how it works" pages, a brand is present from the first day, tasks and rewards are brand-funded, and the advocate knows why she is there [SNIPPET, https://www.duel.tech/how-it-works/ambassadors via `research/duel_customer_evidence.md`]. Sway deliberately removes the brand from the first months. That is a defensible bet, but it means Sway must supply a reason to use the product alone in an empty room, and the package has not written one down.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`: "Return because the Locker, progress, recognition, and rewards are valuable." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`: "Before brands participate, Sway Points should create visible progress and unlock Sway-funded access or rewards." `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "there is a second incentive beyond money: recognition."

**What evidence would settle it.** A sentence from five real members, in their own words, answering "if nobody else was on this, what would you use it for?", followed a week later by whether they did. If the answers are all variants of "to keep a list," the product is a list app until the network exists, and the plan must fund density rather than assume retention.

**Cheapest test.** The "empty room" test. Give five people (not the Wizard of Oz five) a one-page version of their own Sway (any free page tool with their five products and reasons; see `research/alternatives_landscape.md` Test 3), tell them truthfully that nobody else has one yet, and ask them to use it however they like for a week. At day seven ask: what did you use it for, how many times, would you keep it, and what would make you delete it. One founder, five people, start by Tuesday 22 September, report by Friday 2 October. At the workshop: write the single-player value in one sentence before any discussion of points or Trust. If the sentence cannot be written, record Sway as a network product and put density funding into the 90-day plan.

### Objection 4. A visible incentive plus mandatory disclosure changes what a recommendation means between people who know each other

**In one sentence.** The moment a run-club friend can see, or must be told, that the advocate earns from her purchase, the recommendation stops being a favour and becomes a transaction, and the strong-tie trust that Sway's whole thesis rests on is the thing most damaged.

**Why it matters.** Sway's stated advantage over creator platforms is the strong tie: "A runner who never posts may cause half her club to switch shoes" (`02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`). [PRACTITIONER VIEW] Strong-tie recommendations are credible precisely because nothing is in it for the recommender; `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 3 says the same: "Any visible commission, points or 'pass' changes the social meaning of the act. Whether that change is acceptable in a run club is unknown and must be tested, not assumed." The academic literature on referral rewards is consistent with this concern: Ryu and Feick's 2007 Journal of Marketing paper ("A Penny for Your Thoughts: Referral Reward Programs and Referral Likelihood") is generally cited as finding that rewards raise referral likelihood more for weak ties than for strong ties, and more for weaker brands than for stronger ones [NEEDS SOURCE: the paper could not be fetched in this session; the founders should read the abstract before citing it]. The disclosure obligation makes the incentive visible whether Sway wants it or not: UK advertising rules require affiliate relationships to be made clear to the consumer, and US FTC endorsement guidance requires material connections to be disclosed; `research/investor_lens.md` 6.2 lists the exact texts as [UNVERIFIED] pending a lawyer's read, but the principle is not in doubt. So the recipient page must say something like "Tory may earn a small commission," and that line sits directly under "why Tory loves it."

The guardrail in `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` ("Do not make the experience feel like couponing or a transactional affiliate utility") is in tension with EARN being step five of the twelve-step spine and "Affiliate" being one of the two Trust views in `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`. [INFERENCE] The documents want the money to be present but not felt. In a strong-tie community, the money is felt the first time it is disclosed.

One counterpoint to be fair: for creator-to-audience relationships, "Use my code EMMA20" reportedly "feels like identity" and like "supporting that person directly" [SNIPPET, https://www.rewardful.com/articles/affiliate-coupon-codes-vs-influencer-links via `research/duel_customer_evidence.md`]. That is a parasocial relationship, not a friendship. Whether the same holds across a table at a run club is exactly the open question.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` spine: "RECOMMEND → EARN → TRUST." `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`: "Tori sees observable outcomes and any eligible pending/confirmed/reversed affiliate earnings." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`: "Validate what members value; avoid a discount-only feeling."

**What evidence would settle it.** A paired comparison from ten recipients: the same recommendation page with and without a disclosure line, and a rating of how much they trust the recommendation and how likely they are to click. And from ten advocates: "Would you recommend differently if your friend knew you earned four dollars?" with the reasons recorded verbatim.

**Cheapest test.** Two paper or single-screen versions of the recipient page (real product, real reason, one with "Tory may earn a small commission from this link," one without). Ten people at one community session, five see each version first, then both; ask which they trust more and why, and whether they would tap through. Fifteen minutes each, one founder, by Friday 2 October. Ask the ten advocates in the My 5 conversations question 4 from `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` 6b ("When you share a discount code with a friend, how does it feel? When does it feel wrong?") and add: "Would you want your friend to know you earned from it?" At the workshop: decide the default. Earnings hidden from the recipient and off by default for the advocate (money is a later opt-in) is the option most consistent with the guardrail; earnings shown is the option most consistent with the spine. The founders cannot have both, and the decision changes the first screen of the Locker (audit tension 5.4).

### Objection 5. Points and "recognition" are the wrong motivator for the people Sway most wants, and points will be gamed in a way that pollutes Trust

**In one sentence.** The person everyone asks is motivated by being right, being useful and being asked, not by an airline-style status bar or by the possibility that HOKA notices her months from now, while the people who are motivated by brand recognition are proto-creators whom ShopMy and LTK already serve; and any point awarded for "get your first IRL scan" will be earned by scanning a partner's phone on the sofa.

**Why it matters.** Airline status works because the behaviour (flying) already happens and the status redeems into something with cash value (upgrades, lounges) [PRACTITIONER VIEW]. Sway Points reward new behaviour and redeem into Sway-funded rewards with no budget (objection 3). The Founding Member examples in `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` ("add your five things," "get your first IRL scan," "bring in someone whose taste you trust") are exactly the actions that can be performed without any genuine recommendation taking place. [INFERENCE] If Points reward scans and Trust is built from scans, then the two currencies the language discipline insists are different will be fed by the same faked event, and the Trust Map's first "surprising" pattern will be couples and flatmates. Advocate-side evidence in the category says advocates value free or discounted product first (BrandChamp survey, "39% of ambassadors surveyed joined a program for free or discounted products" [VENDOR-PUBLISHED, https://brandchamp.io/blog/rewards-that-make-ambassadors-happy/]) and complain about "rewards for certain missions [being] low compared to the effort required" [SNIPPET, Brandbassador Google Play reviews via `research/duel_customer_evidence.md`]. Nothing in the public record retrieved says non-creators are motivated by being noticed by a brand; the package's line "HOKA notices" is a founder belief [HYPOTHESIS].

Again, in fairness, Duel's public model (points and tiers inside a brand's own programme, funded by that brand) [SNIPPET, https://www.duel.tech/how-it-works/ambassadors] makes recognition immediate and concrete. Sway's version delays it by design.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md` Progress: "Use an airline-status-style visual." `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "You love HOKA. You recommend HOKA. People actually listen to you. HOKA notices." `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` Founding Member examples and "Points create momentum. Trust must be earned." `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`: "'Trust' is evidence that people act because of a recommendation; it is not merely activity points."

**What evidence would settle it.** A forced ranking from ten community members of four rewards of equal cost to Sway (a small cash-equivalent commission, a local class or race entry, a named message from a brand they love, and their name shown as "most asked about shoes" inside their own club), plus their account of the last time a brand contacted them and what they did next. And, from the Wizard of Oz week, the share of scans that occurred between the same two people.

**Cheapest test.** Add the forced ranking to the intercept conversations already scoped in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` Gap 5 (Blake, two evenings, by Friday 3 October), with the fourth option (in-club recognition) included because it is the one the package never considers. In the Wizard of Oz tally, record who scanned; count repeat pairs. At the workshop: either remove Points from the Alpha entirely, or restrict them to actions that cannot be faked without a second household (a recipient who saves with a new email, a recipient who later scans someone else), and write a one-line rule that a repeated scan between the same pair counts once for Trust.

### Objection 6. The recipient-to-member loop is structurally weak because the recipient's job is finished before Sway asks anything of them

**In one sentence.** A recipient who scans wants one thing, the product her friend has, and the page gives it to her at step one, so the invitations to "save with email" and "join Sway to keep scans, earn Sway Points, and build a Locker" arrive after her motivation has been spent and ask her to become a different kind of person.

**Why it matters.** `research/context_audit.md` 2.5 notes that recipient-to-member conversion "is the only organic growth loop described; without it, acquisition depends entirely on community outreach." [PRACTITIONER VIEW] Recipient-side loops work when the recipient's own action requires an account (payments, shared documents, group chats) or when the shared artefact is co-created. Neither is true here: buying HOKA shoes does not require a Sway account, and the Locker is one person's. The package is right not to force a download ("Do not force an app download before the recipient understands the recommendation and value," `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`), but the consequence is that the loop's coefficient will be near zero unless the page gives the recipient a reason to stay on Sway rather than leave to the retailer. The referral gap figure in the category ("83% of satisfied customers are willing to refer... yet only 29% actually do" [THIRD-PARTY, methodology not retrieved, https://growsurf.com/statistics/referral-marketing-statistics/]) is about referrers, not recipients; there is no recipient-side conversion benchmark in the package or the sibling files [NEEDS SOURCE].

**What in the documents triggered it.** `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md` recipient journey: "1. Continue to the product or claim the available benefit. 2. Optionally save the product using email. 3. Optionally join/download Sway." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` metrics: "Recipient-to-member conversion."

**What evidence would settle it.** From ten people shown a real recipient page cold: what they tap first, whether anyone taps "join" unprompted, and what they say when asked "what would make you want one of these yourself?" A count of zero unprompted joins out of ten is a finding, not a failure; it says the loop must be redesigned.

**Cheapest test.** `research/prototype_feasibility.md` already builds the recipient page first; before the workshop, mock it in any page tool with one real product and one real reason and show it cold to ten people (strangers are better than friends), recording first tap and the verbatim answer to "what would make you want your own?" Test two second screens: (a) the brand's product page, as designed; (b) "see the rest of Tory's Sway," which keeps the recipient inside the advocate's list the way a shared playlist keeps a listener inside the platform [PRACTITIONER VIEW]. One founder, ten people, by Friday 2 October. At the workshop: decide what the recipient sees second, and whether the growth loop is the recipient at all or the advocate's list travelling through group chats (see alternative A).

### Objection 7. Wellness people share privately, about products that are often mundane or intimate, and a public taste profile is a creator behaviour

**In one sentence.** The Sway Link ("one digital destination to send when someone asks") and the public profile assume a person wants a standing, public record of her taste, when the products wellness-community members actually swear by (electrolytes, socks, chafe balm, supplements, sleep aids, skincare) are shared one-to-one, in private, and often only when asked.

**Why it matters.** The only figures in the package's orbit on this are dated and third-party: a HubSpot survey re-cited as 61 per cent preferring to share privately, and a 2016 RadiumOne figure of 84 per cent of sharing via "dark social" [THIRD-PARTY, dated, https://paxcom.ai/blog/dark-social-for-ecommerce-brands/ via `research/investor_lens.md`]. They should not be cited as market sizing, but the direction is consistent with ordinary observation and with the "doing nothing" description in `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 3 (the recipient gets "a brand URL, an Amazon link, or a screenshot into WhatsApp"). [INFERENCE] The incumbents that succeed with public profiles gate them by audience: LTK requires "public social profiles and engaged followings" for creators [SNIPPET, https://company.shopltk.com/en-gb/faqs], and ShopMy makes Circles searchable only above Trendsetter or Icon status or a Trusted By count above 100 [SNIPPET, https://guide.shopmy.us/circles-wishlist-latest-finds-and-more/5jLY2jskGAENwEWJEf147B/faqs/5kdVRoGw9nmmppW3uMKyRZ]. Sway's promise is to serve the people below those gates, and those are the people least likely to want a public page. The Locker as "a living record of your taste" (`02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`) is a creator's asset; a run-club member's asset is the answer to a question, sent once.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`: "My Sway Link: One digital destination to send when someone asks for recommendations" and "A beautiful, shareable list." `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` MVP core: "public profile/recommendation." `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`: "Product-specific recommendation pages."

**What evidence would settle it.** For every product in the ten My 5 lists, the answer to "would you put this on a page under your name that anyone could open, or would you only ever send it to a specific person?" and a count of the split. And from the Wizard of Oz week, the ratio of whole-list shares to single-item shares.

**Cheapest test.** Add the public-or-private question to the My 5 conversations (objection 2's test), item by item, and record the reason for each "private." Count how many respondents' lists are at least half "private." In the Wizard of Oz week, record whether each use was the whole page or one item. Report by Friday 2 October. At the workshop: decide the default visibility (private answer bank with per-item sharing, or public profile), because it changes the Locker's information architecture and the entire recipient page.

### Objection 8. Community leaders are gatekeepers with competing incentives, and "density" needs more members per node than the plan's numbers give

**In one sentence.** Run-club captains and studio owners already trade access to their members for brand perks, so a third party that individualises and monetises members' recommendations is a threat to the captain's own deal and to the club's "no selling to members" norm, and the plan's 250 to 500 members "across a handful of dense wellness communities" spreads too thin for any recommendation to land on another member.

**Why it matters.** The acquisition model rules out paid media ("Do not target 'wellness consumers' broadly with paid media," `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`) and rests entirely on leaders granting access. The public record shows leaders already monetise: Heylo offers "sponsor perks" for paying members [SNIPPET, https://www.heylo.com/running-club]; Strava positions clubs as "a brand's home on Strava" with sponsored challenges [SNIPPET, https://business.strava.com/resources/ultimate-guide-sponsored-challenges]; direct run-club sponsorship guides exist for brands [THIRD-PARTY, https://analog.directory/blog/posts/run-club-sponsorship-for-brands] (all via `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 2). Studios often sell product at the desk (grip socks, apparel, supplements) [PRACTITIONER VIEW; NEEDS SOURCE for any share], so a member's Locker recommending a competing brand sends money out the door. [INFERENCE] The captain's question will not be "what do my members get?" but "what do I get, and does this change how my club feels?"

On density: the Trust Map needs propagation between members, which means recipient and advocate in the same node. A club of 200 with 50 Sway members has 25 per cent penetration; most "what do you use?" questions will land on non-members, which produces scans and links but not the person-to-person graph the brand pitch depends on. `research/context_audit.md` 2.10 notes nobody has done this arithmetic. The plan's own numbers (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`: 250 to 500 members across "a handful" of communities in Phase 1) imply 50 to 100 per node at best.

**What in the documents triggered it.** `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`: "Recruit a small set of Founding Members inside dense wellness communities in one city... Build density rather than spreading nationally." `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` Week 1: "Contact 5-10 community leaders and attend at least three gatherings."

**What evidence would settle it.** Three leaders' verbatim responses to the pitch, what each asks for in return, and whether any objects to members earning; plus one hour of observation at a session counting how many product questions are asked and answered aloud [PRACTITIONER VIEW: if it is fewer than two per hour, the "high frequency" claim in the node table is wrong for that node].

**Cheapest test.** The three-leader contact already proposed in `research/context_audit.md` 2.9, with two added questions: "What do brands give you or the club today?" and "Would a member earning a small commission from recommending shoes to another member be a problem for you?" Then one founder sits through one full session at one node with a notebook and counts product questions. Three calls and one evening, by Friday 2 October. At the workshop: choose node types where the leader has no competing retail and something to gain (for example a run club with no sponsor, a HYROX or strength gym that does not sell product), set a per-node penetration target rather than a city total, and write the one-line offer to the leader.

## 2. What must be true for Sway to work, from this lens

Each statement is written so that it can be false. If the founders cannot say how they would know, it belongs on the evidence-gap list, not in the deck.

1. **In a real recommendation moment, using Sway is no slower than saying the brand name and sending a link later, and the recipient gets something the brand's own site does not**: the exact variant and size, the reason, and where the advocate actually bought it. If the page is just a prettier link to the retailer, the Pass will not be used and the Link will not be preferred over a pasted URL.

2. **The advocate gets a private, single-player value on day one, with no recipient and no brand present**, and can say what it is in one sentence. Candidates: the list replaces Notes and Instagram saves; the page ends the "what was that thing you told me about?" re-asking; the record of what she has told whom. Retention in months one to six depends on this alone.

3. **The Locker is reachable at the moment the trigger fires** (share sheet, Wallet, WhatsApp, a card in a group chat), because the trigger fires outside any app and a home-screen icon will not be remembered.

4. **A visible incentive does not reduce trust between people who know each other, or Sway leads with helpfulness and keeps money off by default.** One of these must hold; the disclosure line on the recipient page makes it impossible to have money present but unfelt.

5. **At least a stated share of the products in the first hundred Lockers are wellness-brand products that a wellness brand would recognise as its own** [the founders set the threshold; my suggestion is 40 per cent, PRACTITIONER VIEW]. Otherwise the Trust Map at 90 days is an Amazon list and there is no brand to approach.

6. **Recipients act at a rate materially above a pasted brand link, and some ask for their own list without prompting.** If neither is true, growth is founder outreach only, and the 90-day plan must be costed as such.

7. **At least one community node reaches a penetration where recommendations land on other members** [suggestion: 30 per cent of active members within 60 days, PRACTITIONER VIEW], with the leader's active support, because the graph needs edges inside a node, not Lockers scattered across a city. And the founders can be physically present at that node every week, because in the first months the founders are the retention engine, not the app.

## 3. Stronger alternatives and reframings

Each keeps the parts of the thesis that remove effort (real products, genuine reasons, no follower gate, honest attribution, wellness communities as the place to start) and changes the part that adds effort or changes social meaning. Each has its own biggest risk; none is free.

### Alternative A. Start as a WhatsApp-native recommendation card ("Send my Sway"), with the scan as a fallback

**The alternative.** The unit of the product is a card, not a profile. When someone asks, the advocate opens Sway from the share sheet or a keyboard shortcut, taps the product, and sends a card into the chat where the question was asked: image, product name and variant, one line of why, one link that resolves through Sway. The Locker is her history of cards sent. The Pass is a QR of the same card for the moment when the question is asked face to face and the person is not in her contacts. The recipient never needs an app; the card's link is the recipient page already designed in `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`. A second mechanic, "Ask my Sway," lets a person post a question ("what electrolyte do you use?") to a friend or a group and receive cards back, so the loop can be started by the person with the need, who is the more motivated party.

**What it changes.** Product: the hero is the share sheet and the card, not the Pass and the profile; no consumer app download is needed on either side for the Alpha; the prototype's build order flips to card generator, then recipient page, then Locker, then QR. Sales: the brand line becomes "we can see recommendations that happen in group chats and in person, and who they reach," which is a claim no link tool makes and which addresses the survey-versus-pixel gap noted in `research/investor_lens.md` 6.1 (row 3, [SNIPPET, vendor]). Investor story: a dark-social attribution layer with a consumer-owned record; the comparison set is link tools and referral tools, and the differentiation is the reason-plus-person-plus-recipient record rather than the mechanic.

**What it keeps.** Locker, Sway Link, recipient page, the attribution chain, wellness communities as acquisition nodes, one identity, EARN as a later opt-in. The spine's words survive; the order of emphasis changes.

**Biggest risk.** It sits closer to a commodity. Beacons and Linktree already make a five-product page with a link in minutes ([SNIPPET, https://beacons.ai/i/pricing; https://linktr.ee/features/shops] via `research/alternatives_landscape.md`), WhatsApp link previews are limited, and recipient identity is weak unless the recipient saves or signs up. The defensibility has to come from the record (who sent what to whom and what happened), and that record is thin until volume exists.

### Alternative B. A private "bought it, would buy again" ledger, shared one item at a time

**The alternative.** The single-player value is a personal record of what she has bought and whether she would buy it again, with the Locker being the "would buy again" subset. Entry is by search, by photo, or, where feasible later, by importing order confirmations. Everything is private by default; sharing is per item, to a person or a group, and each share creates the same attributable recommendation. My 5 becomes the first five verdicts rather than a public list.

**What it changes.** Product: verdicts (buy again, would not) instead of a curated profile; privacy first; sharing is the second act, not the first; the recipient page gains one line of honest signal ("Tory has bought this twice"). Sales: brands get a cross-brand, honest repurchase-intent signal from real customers, which is a different and arguably more valuable dataset than "who has followers," and one no single-brand loyalty tool holds; the intelligence package in the PDFs becomes "who would buy you again, who would not, and who they tell." Investor story: a consumer data asset whose retention is tied to purchasing, which people do anyway (this is the airline-status logic applied correctly, since the behaviour already happens), with recommendation and attribution layered on top.

**What it keeps.** Real products and genuine reasons, the Locker as home, per-item Sway Links and the Pass, the attribution chain, wellness as the launch territory, the Trust Map as the long-term asset.

**Biggest risk.** Import is technically and legally hard (email access, retailer terms) and out of reach for the Alpha, so the ledger starts manual, and manual purchase logging is a chore most people abandon [PRACTITIONER VIEW; NEEDS SOURCE for any abandonment figure]. Negative verdicts also make brands nervous, and the product's frequency is still purchase frequency, so the trigger problem in objection 2 is reduced rather than solved.

### Alternative C. A group product: the club's kit list, with each item attributed to the member who added it

**The alternative.** The unit is the community, not the person. A run club, studio or gym gets a shared list, "what our club swears by," where each item carries the name and reason of the member who added it. New members are onboarded through the club's list (the captain's welcome message links to it). Individual Lockers are the member's own items across every group she belongs to. The captain is the distribution partner and administrator, and club-level benefits (a brand's product seeding, a race entry pool) flow through Sway to the club rather than competing with the captain's existing sponsor deals. The first "Collective" is a club, not a brand.

**What it changes.** Product: group lists, membership, member attribution within a group, a captain role, and notifications when a clubmate adds or asks; the Pass becomes the club's QR at the coffee table as well as the member's. Sales: sell to leaders first as a free tool with a visible benefit to them, then to brands as access to named clubs with visible product density ("your community already exists" becomes literally true, one club at a time). Investor story: community-led growth with a natural loop (every new member of the club is a new user), a B2B2C shape, and a defensible per-community graph that brand-scoped referral tools and creator platforms do not hold.

**What it keeps.** Person-level attribution, real products and reasons, wellness density, no follower gate, the recipient page, and the long-term Trust Map, now built community by community.

**Biggest risk.** The captain is now the single point of failure: if the captain declines or leaves, the node dies. Group lists go stale without a curator, and members may feel the club is being sold to. Objection 8 applies with more force, not less, and the offer to the leader has to be worked out before anything else.

### Alternative D. Creator-lite entry: recruit only "the person everyone asks" in each community

**The alternative.** Instead of onboarding every member, identify the three to five people per node whom others already ask (by asking members "who do you ask about shoes?"), and give those people a tool for the questions they answer repeatedly: an "ask me" page with their standing answers ("my answer on shoes for wide feet," "my answer on electrolytes"), each answer being a Locker item with a reason. Everyone else is a recipient first and becomes an advocate only if they start being asked.

**What it changes.** Product: fewer, deeper Lockers; standing answers rather than a flat list; a request-and-answer mechanic; recognition inside the community ("most asked about shoes at [club]") rather than from a brand. Sales: brands are offered the twenty most-asked people in a city, with evidence of who asks them and what happens next; this is the run-club-shoe-switcher example from `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` made operational. Investor story: a micro-expert network below the follower gate, with a clear answer to "why not ShopMy" (these people have no audience and want none) and a clearer path to brand recognition, because the advocates are few and legible.

**What it keeps.** No follower gate, IRL and digital attribution, the Locker, the Pass and Link, wellness communities, the Trust Map.

**Biggest risk.** Small numbers, slow growth, and the possibility that the most-asked people are exactly the people brands already seed with product, so they may not want commission and may already have a brand relationship that Sway would be seen to interfere with. The "everyone can have influence" positioning narrows to "some people have influence," which is truer and less inspiring.

### A note on combining them

[INFERENCE] A and D combine cleanly (a card-based tool given first to the most-asked people), and either can grow into C once a node reaches density. B is a different bet on where value comes from and should be chosen, not blended, because "private ledger" and "shareable identity" pull the first screen in opposite directions.

## 4. Discussion prompts for the workshop, verbatim

These are written to be read aloud by one founder to the other. Each should be answered in writing for five minutes before discussion, as the brief's design principles require.

1. "Describe the last three times someone asked you what product you use. Where were you, what did you say, what happened in the next sixty seconds, and at which second, if any, would you have pulled out a QR code?"

2. "If nobody else ever joined Sway, what would you personally use your own Locker for next week, and how many times?"

3. "Which of your five products would you put on a public page under your name, and which would you only ever send to one person? Why the difference?"

4. "Your run-club friend buys the shoes you recommended and later learns you earned four dollars from it. Say out loud what she says to you, and what you say back."

5. "Where does Sway live at the exact moment someone asks you a question: the home screen, the share sheet, Wallet, the WhatsApp keyboard, a card in your wallet? Pick one, and say what you are giving up by not picking the others."

6. "Which single action earns the first Sway Point, and how would two people who live together earn it without a real recommendation ever happening?"

7. "What does the recipient see second: the brand's product page, or the rest of the advocate's Sway? Which of those makes Sway grow, and which makes the advocate earn?"

8. "Name the captain of one specific run club or the owner of one specific studio you would call on Monday 12 October. What does that person get from saying yes, in their words rather than ours?"

9. "Out of the first hundred Lockers, what share of products will be wellness brands a brand marketer would recognise, and what share will be headphones, vacuum cleaners and Amazon supplements? If it is mostly the second, who do we pitch in Phase 4?"

10. "What is the one number from the first cohort that would make us stop and change the mechanic: scans per member per month, share of moments where the Pass was used, unprompted joins per hundred recipients, or second-month return rate? Set the number now."

11. "Is the hero the Pass or the card? If a stopwatch on the prototype shows the scan takes more than fifteen seconds, do we change the demo, the product, or the pitch?"

12. "What do we say to a brand marketer who asks 'why would my customers want to be measured?' Answer as the customer, not as Sway."

13. "If we removed Points, Progress and Earn from the Alpha entirely and shipped only Locker, card, Pass and the recipient page, what would we lose in the first ninety days, and what would we stop having to explain?"

14. "Duel's public model puts the brand in the room from day one, with tasks and rewards the brand pays for. We are choosing to keep the brand out for months. Say in one sentence what replaces the brand as the reason to keep going, and whether either of us actually believes it."

15. "Which of us will be at the launch node every week for twelve weeks, on which day, and what happens to the pilot if that person cannot?"

## 5. The single question I would ask the founders first

"In the last month, how many times did someone ask either of you what you use, and in how many of those moments did you do anything other than say the name or send a link later?"

The answer tells you whether the behaviour Sway is built on happens often enough to matter, and whether the two people building it would themselves use the mechanic they are asking others to adopt. If the honest answer is "a few times, and we said the name," the workshop should start from that fact and design a product that makes saying the name and sending the link better, rather than one that asks for a scan.

## 6. Where the package is right, from this lens

So that the objections are read as fair rather than dismissive:

- Not defining advocacy by follower count is the correct reading of where trust lives, and the incumbents' gates (LTK's public-profile requirement, ShopMy's search threshold [SNIPPET, cited above]) confirm the space below them is unserved.
- Not forcing a recipient download, putting the product image first, showing who recommends and why, and offering one dominant action is good recipient-page craft and is the part of the product most likely to test well cold.
- "Preserve unknowns" and "Do not infer a purchase from a click" (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`) are the right posture and will read as honest to both members and brands.
- One identity and one Pass across brands is the correct simplification; the mistake would be to make the person carry a code per brand, which is what brand-scoped referral tools already do.
- Starting in dense communities rather than with paid media is right; the objection is to the offer made to the community's leader and to the penetration arithmetic, not to the choice of communities.
- "Make Sway useful before it is a network" (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`) is exactly the right rule. The package has not yet said what the usefulness is, and that is the first thing the workshop should write down.
