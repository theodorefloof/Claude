# Challenge: the brand buyer's lens on Sway

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Barcelona founder workshop (Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026).

**Lens.** Head of community and advocacy marketing at a mid-sized wellness brand (£20M to £200M revenue; running shoes, supplements or skincare). I run an ambassador programme on a platform such as Duel or Brandbassador, I hold a budget that I defend twice a year, and my finance partner believes attributed revenue and very little else.

**What this file is and is not.**

- It is a structured attack on Sway from one buyer's chair, written to make the workshop harder and the sales strategy better.
- It is not customer evidence. No brand was spoken to for this file. Every statement about how "I" buy is a practitioner inference and is marked [INFERENCE]. The founders must replace each one with what real marketers say; the eight brand questions in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 6a are the instrument.
- External facts come from the sibling research files written today, with the URL and evidence label they recorded: [SNIPPET] means a search snippet, primary page not fetched; [VENDOR-PUBLISHED] means a vendor's own case study figure; [THIRD-PARTY], [UNVERIFIED] and [NEEDS SOURCE] as elsewhere in the programme. Network note: this session's web search budget was already exhausted before this file began, and every direct fetch attempted for it (hoka.com, analog.directory, fairing.co, heylo.com) was blocked by the egress proxy. No new external fact was gathered here.
- Everything about Duel comes from public pages only, as cited in `research/duel_public_offering.md` and `research/duel_customer_evidence.md`. No internal Duel material was used.
- The package prices ($30K, $75K, $150K+), the 80/20 split and the 16 to 18 per cent "Collective" rates are the PDFs' own planning assumptions [ILLUSTRATIVE], not offers, and are quoted as such.
- House style: Australian English, no em dashes. Quotations keep their original US spelling.

## 0. The short answer: would I buy Sway, and when?

Not the intelligence package, not in the next budget year, and not as the documents describe it. The honest sequence from my chair is:

1. **A free look: yes, tomorrow,** provided it covers my SKUs and a city where I sell. I would give thirty minutes to anyone who can show me named, consenting people recommending my products in person. I would not pay for that look, because I cannot yet act on it inside the same tool, and because I am the person who has to prove to finance that the money did something.
2. **A small paid study: plausibly,** from a community or insight line, if it is framed as a fixed-term study with a report I can forward to my CFO and a list of people I can invite into the programme I already run. The amount is whatever I can commit without triggering procurement. That threshold varies by company and must be asked, not assumed [INFERENCE].
3. **An activation package: only after** (a) the study taught my team something it did not already know, (b) legal accepted the data arrangement, (c) my affiliate manager accepted Sway as a publisher at a rate I could defend, and (d) I could see how to run a Collective without my two-person team doubling its workload. Realistically that is a second budget year.
4. **A scale package: not a 2027 question.**

**When.** By Sway's own sequence, the first brand conversation comes only after density in one city; `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` puts "first brand conversations" in days 60 to 90 of a plan that has not yet started, and `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` says "Approach brands with proof that an organic advocate community already exists." Brands of my size typically set next year's marketing budget in the second half of the current year [INFERENCE; NEEDS SOURCE for any general claim]. So unless the founders find a smaller, earlier offer that fits inside a line I already have (section 3), the first realistic paid invoice from a brand like mine lands in the financial year after next.

The eight objections that follow are ordered by how much each one would delay or kill a purchase from my chair.

## 1. My eight strongest objections

### Objection 1. There is no budget line for "seeing", and my CFO does not fund insight I cannot act on inside the same tool

**In one sentence.** The intelligence-only package asks me to pay (illustratively $30K a year) for a view of organic advocacy that I can only act on by buying the next package, and no line in my budget exists to fund a view.

**Why it matters.** Intelligence is Sway's first brand revenue layer and the hinge between the consumer phase and the business (`research/context_audit.md` section 4, item 4). If it does not sell, Sway's first brand money is activation, which needs Collective tooling the MVP boundary deliberately excludes.

**What triggered it.** `04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt`: "Bronze - See it... $30K / yr... Continuous Trust Map and advocacy intelligence." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`: "Intelligence: brands pay to understand ongoing organic advocacy and Trust Map signals." `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "Bronze tells the brand what is happening." The public category evidence points the other way. A Duel reviewer names "the difficulty in proving emotional advocacy's financial worth to the business" (G2, September 2024, https://www.g2.com/products/duel/reviews [SNIPPET]). Duel built a "CFO-ready" composite metric, Gross Advocacy Value, in response (https://www.duel.tech/glossary/gross-advocacy-value [SNIPPET]). Measuring ROI is reported as the top challenge in creator marketing ("52% of marketers", https://www.aspire.io/blog/influencer-marketing-challenges-and-solutions [SNIPPET, vendor]). A category that struggles to justify activation spend will not find it easier to justify pure intelligence spend [INFERENCE].

My lines, for reference [INFERENCE]: platform subscription (already spent on the incumbent), product seeding and gifting, creator fees, commissions and rewards, events and field, agency, and sometimes a small "test and learn" pot. "Intelligence" would have to come out of test and learn, out of an insight or research team's agency budget (a different team with its own suppliers), or by displacing something I already defend.

**What evidence would settle it.** Three wellness brand marketers, not prompted with a price, naming (a) the budget line they would use, (b) the internal report or meeting the Trust Map would feed, (c) what they would have to show six months later to renew, and (d) a number they would pay. Two of three saying "I would take it free but could not pay" settles it the other way.

**Cheapest test (next four weeks).** Tory adds to every brand conversation already scoped in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` Gap 3 (three to five conversations, complete by Wednesday 7 October): "Show me the last new vendor under £25K you signed. Which line paid, who approved it, and what did it have to prove by month six?" Then: "If I could show you the people recommending you in person in one city, what would you do with that list on Monday, and what is it worth?" Quote no Sway price. At the workshop: decide whether intelligence is a paid package at all, or a free sales instrument that opens the activation conversation (section 3, options A and E).

### Objection 2. "We found your community" will, at pilot scale, mean a few dozen people; I will not take the meeting until it beats what I already know

**In one sentence.** The pitch "Your community already exists. We found it." assumes Sway's sample in one city is larger and more surprising than my CRM, my programme membership, my field team's run-club relationships and my post-purchase survey, and nothing in the package estimates whether it can be.

**Why it matters.** It is the brand pitch (`04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`, `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` Phase 4) and the moment the consumer phase is meant to turn into revenue. `research/context_audit.md` 2.10 records that nobody has yet done the maths on how much activity a cohort needs before any pattern is more than noise.

**What triggered it.** `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`: "As soon as the data becomes surprising, take the meeting." The ramp figures in the same PDF ("250-500 Founding Members", "750-1,500 members") are [ILLUSTRATIVE] and the current strategy deliberately sets no number. Against that, the scale I am used to seeing from my platform category: Duel's published case studies cite "16,608 members (as of Sep 2023)" for Monica Vinader and "more than 3,000" for Beekman 1802 (https://www.duel.tech/case-studies/monica-vinader ; https://www.duel.tech/case-studies/beekman-1802 [VENDOR-PUBLISHED]). I also already have a "how did you hear about us" survey that tells me word of mouth is under-counted; the public example is "2% in Platform Data versus 23% in Survey Data" (https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide [SNIPPET, vendor]). So the existence of an invisible community is not news to me; what would be news is who, specifically, and whether they are people I do not already have.

Three specific gaps from my chair [INFERENCE]:

- **Coverage of my SKUs.** "My 5" rewards hero products people already own. My launch calendar needs the new SKU. Nothing in the MVP boundary syncs my catalogue or gives a SKU-level view, so a Locker full of my three-year-old hero shoe tells me what I already know.
- **Non-customers.** The interesting people are the ones not in my CRM. Sway cannot tell me that without matching against my CRM under consent, which is the data arrangement in Objection 5.
- **Density.** A hundred members spread across five community nodes gives me perhaps a handful holding my brand, each with a couple of recipients. I cannot report that.

**What evidence would settle it.** A coverage table for one brand in one city: members with the brand in their Locker; which SKUs; distinct recipients; actions by type (scan, view, save, click, purchase where visible); share of members and recipients matched to the brand's CRM with consent; and the brand's own survey share for word of mouth as the comparison. Plus a "surprise threshold" agreed with a real marketer before the pilot, not after.

**Cheapest test.** At the workshop (Day 2, pilot criteria and 30/60/90-day tests): write the surprise threshold in numbers, using the audit's formula ("at least N distinct products each recommended by at least M members to at least K distinct recipients in one community"), then compute what cohort size and event rate would reach it in 90 days. If a 100-member cohort cannot, move the brand-approach date in the plan rather than the threshold. Before the workshop: Tory asks each marketer, "What is the smallest number of verified in-person recommenders of your brand in one city that would get you to (a) a meeting and (b) a purchase order?"

### Objection 3. A scan is softer than the affiliate revenue I already report; unless Sway's events reconcile to my orders and show incrementality, finance will file them under EMV

**In one sentence.** Observable events (scan, view, save, click) are honest, but they are further from revenue than the link-and-code attribution my finance partner already discounts, and the MVP has no way to reconcile them to my order data.

**Why it matters.** IRL attribution is Sway's most specific claim to novelty (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`: "A Sway Pass is the real-world counterpart of an affiliate link"). If a budget holder cannot turn it into a number a CFO accepts, it is a feature the consumer enjoys, not something a brand pays for.

**What triggered it.** `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`: the chain ends at "purchase, where visible" and "Do not infer a purchase from a click." `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`: "Do not claim that attribution is perfect." `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` puts "enterprise integrations" in later layers. Yet `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` page 1 says "Sway can attribute the sale and share the economics" (the audit flags this tension at 3.17). Category context: EMV has "no industry standard formula" (https://www.aspire.io/blog/is-emv-bs-rethinking-influencer-marketing-roi [SNIPPET]); `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` 4c infers that "The only metric a CFO reliably accepts is attributed revenue." The one public fact that helps Sway is the survey-versus-pixel gap above.

Two further problems from my chair [INFERENCE]:

- **Incrementality.** Friends of my advocates at a run club are disproportionately already my customers. If Sway-attributed purchases are mostly existing customers buying through a friend's link, I am paying commission on sales I would have had anyway. My referral programme already fights this; Sway's honest event list does not fix it if the "purchase" column is populated by my own affiliate network's cookie.
- **Double counting.** A Sway Link carrying affiliate parameters and a Sway Pass scan followed by an in-store purchase are two different claims. The first competes with my other publishers for last click; the second is invisible to me unless the retailer or my POS knows about it.

**What evidence would settle it.** For one pilot brand: a manual reconciliation of Sway events to the brand's own orders (even a spreadsheet), with a new-versus-existing customer flag, order value and return rate, set beside the brand's post-purchase survey share for word of mouth. And an answer from the brand's marketer to "which single number does your CFO believe?"

**Cheapest test.** Before the workshop: questions 3 and 5 from `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` 6a, plus "Do you run a post-purchase survey, and what share of answers say friend, coach or community?" At the workshop: the required output "attribution event definitions" gets two extra columns: "what a brand's finance team could audit, and how, in a pilot without an integration" and "what we will never claim".

### Objection 4. In my affiliate programme, Sway is a publisher sending friends-and-family traffic; my affiliate manager caps that, and I have never paid 16 to 18 per cent to a non-creator

**In one sentence.** The commerce layer makes Sway a new publisher type in my affiliate programme, and the model's rates are set by creator-commerce benchmarks that wellness brands do not pay ordinary customers.

**Why it matters.** EARN is step five of the canonical spine, "Affiliate" is one of the two Trust views, and "monetise from day one" is an investor point. If brands like mine approve Sway only at the base rate with restrictions, EARN becomes a token line and the "not couponing" guardrail is strained by tiny commissions (`research/context_audit.md` section 4, item 3).

**What triggered it.** `04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt`: "For brands that are simply commissionable through an affiliate network, Sway uses the available program rate"; "Collective baseline Target ~16-18%"; "Priority product / launch Example: 20%+". The public rates in the launch category are far lower: HOKA's own affiliate page is reported at 5 per cent full price and 3 per cent sale (https://www.hoka.com/en/us/hoka-affiliate-program.html [SNIPPET]); Amazon health and personal care about 1 per cent (https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/ [THIRD-PARTY, UNVERIFIED]); supplements 8 to 12 per cent direct (https://getlasso.co/niche/supplements/ [THIRD-PARTY]). Awin charges advertisers a network fee whose rate is not published (https://www.awin.com/us/faqs [SNIPPET]). The 16 to 20 per cent band comes from LTK's guidance to brands about creators (https://onbrand.shopltk.com/how-to-choose-the-right-commission-rate-for-influencers [SNIPPET]); `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 5 found "no wellness brand... publicly paying it to non-creators."

From my chair [INFERENCE]: affiliate programme terms commonly restrict incentivised traffic, self-referral and coupon or cashback publishers, and my agency asks of any new publisher "what are you: content, referral, sub-network?" Sub-network status means extra scrutiny [NEEDS SOURCE for the specific terms of the three brands the founders will target]. Code leakage is a standing worry: "Almost all brands running a referral or affiliate program with discount codes will see a leak" (https://www.socialsnowball.io/post/how-to-identify-and-stop-leaked-affiliate-codes [SNIPPET, vendor]). The Honey episode, in which an extension was found "swapping the creator's affiliate tracking for its own at checkout", made every affiliate manager warier of intermediaries who insert themselves before the purchase (https://digiday.com/marketing/the-honey-scandal-is-a-wake-up-call-for-the-creator-industrys-affiliate-partnerships/ [SNIPPET]). I would approve Sway at my base rate, flagged as friends-and-family traffic, if asked; I would not boost to 16 to 18 per cent without evidence of a new-customer share.

**What evidence would settle it.** The public affiliate terms of three target wellness brands on publisher types and incentivised traffic; one affiliate manager's stated approval conditions and rate; the arithmetic of one £100 sale at the real rate with a network fee; and a pilot's new-customer share.

**Cheapest test.** Tory reads the public affiliate programme terms of three wellness brands (their own pages or network listings) and records publisher-type rules, rate and cookie window; one hour, by Friday 2 October. Blake models one £100 sale at 5 per cent under 80/20 with an assumed and labelled network fee (the audit's 2.16 test): if Sway's 20 per cent is under £1, the split is a principle, not a revenue line, at pilot scale. At the workshop: decide whether EARN is in the first proof at all, or whether the Alpha shows only honest eligibility labels (audit 5.14).

### Objection 5. Sway holds the identity, the consent and the data about my customers' friends; my legal team, my platform choice and my brand team all push the other way

**In one sentence.** A Collective inside Sway means my advocate's identity, history and the records about people they recommended to sit with a third party, in an app where the same person's Locker also holds my competitor, and that is the opposite of why I chose my current platform.

**Why it matters.** It is non-negotiable 7 (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`), and `research/context_audit.md` 2.19 and 5.13 already mark "who owns the advocate?" as the first objection Tory will hear. It is also a procurement blocker in its own right (Objection 6).

**What triggered it.** `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`: "The underlying advocate identity and attribution system remain shared with Sway"; "no competitive products in the recipient-facing branded flow" (but one master Locker across brands); geography "when consented". Public praise for the incumbent I use: "Duel lets you own the relationship with your ambassadors and that it's platform-agnostic" (https://us.fitgap.com/products/034686/duel ; https://www.affililist.com/software/duel [SNIPPET]). `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 7 infers that "Brands strongly value owning the relationship and data." On the data side, a recipient who scans but never joins is a data subject; the relevant principles are purpose limitation and data minimisation under UK GDPR Article 5(1) (`research/investor_lens.md` 4.4; [UNVERIFIED wording, primary pages blocked]).

Three separate people in my organisation say no, for different reasons [INFERENCE]:

- **Legal**: wants a data processing agreement, a lawful basis for a record about a non-customer created by a scan, retention periods, and to know where data is hosted.
- **Brand**: will not put my Collective inside an app where Sway's brand sits above mine and the advocate's Locker holds my competitor; my paid ambassador agreements often carry category exclusivity, though community-tier terms usually do not [INFERENCE; NEEDS SOURCE].
- **Programme owner (me)**: does not want a second identity for advocates already in my programme, and needs to know who they belong to when a contract ends.

**What evidence would settle it.** A one-page "who owns what" specification (identity, contact permission, consent records, export, deletion, what the brand keeps when the contract ends) and a draft DPA reviewed by one brand's DPO or lawyer; and two brand marketers answering the exclusivity question directly.

**Cheapest test.** At the workshop: write the one-paragraph answer to "who owns the advocate?" (audit 5.13) and a one-page data map (what is collected, why, retention, who sees it; `research/investor_lens.md` 4.4). Before the workshop: question 8 from 6a plus "Would you run a community whose member identity and data sit with a third party, and under what conditions?" and "Do your ambassador terms ban promoting competitors, and would a cross-brand Locker break them?"

### Objection 6. Procurement cannot onboard Sway as it stands, and the illustrative packages sit above what anyone can sign alone

**In one sentence.** A pre-company with no engineer, no security or privacy documentation, a prototype on a third-party subdomain and one founder employed by my current vendor's category is not a supplier I can put through onboarding, and the $30K to $150K+ package structure prices Sway straight into procurement.

**Why it matters.** Every earlier objection can be argued; this one is a checklist. It also sets the price band of the first offer: below a company-specific threshold a marketer can sign; above it, the sale takes months and needs documents Sway does not have [INFERENCE].

**What triggered it.** `research/context_audit.md` 6.2: the package is silent on incorporation, cap table, budget and founder hours; `06_SOURCE_INDEX.md`: the prototype lives at a `chatgpt.site` subdomain whose account is [UNVERIFIED]. `research/investor_lens.md` 4.2 sets out the disclosure item that Blake is VP of Product at Duel. From my chair that is not a legal point (not mine to judge) but a relationship point: if a founder works at the vendor category I already buy from, my first call is to my account manager, not to Sway [INFERENCE]. `04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt` prices in USD; the audit (3.13) notes currency and first market are undecided.

What procurement asks above the threshold, in my experience [INFERENCE]: company registration, insurance certificates, a security questionnaire, a DPA, references, and a 30 to 90 day cycle. None of it is exotic; all of it is absent.

**What evidence would settle it.** A registered company, a standard contract, a DPA template, insurance, a named support contact; one brand's actual vendor-onboarding checklist; and the threshold below which a marketer can sign alone, from three brands.

**Cheapest test.** Before the workshop: Tory asks each marketer, "What is the largest amount you can commit without procurement, and what does procurement demand above it?" At the workshop: fix company formation and who holds the domain, the prototype and any IP (audit 6.2 items 3 and 4); set a date for a DPA template; and set the first offer's price band relative to the answers, not to the PDF tiers.

### Objection 7. "Wellness brands" is not a buyer; the roles that would use Sway have different KPIs and budgets, and nothing names the trigger that makes one of them buy this quarter

**In one sentence.** The buyer-facing line addresses "wellness brands" as if a brand were a person, but the community manager, the affiliate lead, growth, field marketing, insight and the CMO each hold different money for different reasons, and the package never says which one Sway is for or what event opens their wallet.

**Why it matters.** The brief's question 3 demands "user, buyer, budget holder, purchase trigger". Without a trigger, Sway is a "nice to have" that survives every meeting and never gets bought [INFERENCE].

**What triggered it.** `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`: "Sway helps wellness brands turn real customers and community members into measurable advocates." `04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` sets out four revenue layers and no trigger. `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "Then the brand conversation changes completely", which assumes the meeting turns into a purchase. Duel's own event copy addresses "brand, performance, influencer, affiliate, and creator marketing" leaders and "global CMOs" (https://www.duel.tech/bas-ny-2025 [SNIPPET]), which shows how fragmented this buying centre is even for an established vendor.

A buyer map from my chair [INFERENCE, to be replaced by what marketers say]:

| Role | KPIs they report | Money they hold | What Sway layer speaks to them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community or ambassador manager | members, activation rate, content pieces, referred revenue | rewards, seeding, a small tools line | activation; a recruitment feed |
| Influencer or affiliate lead | ROAS, commission efficiency, new-customer share | commissions, creator fees, network fees | the commerce layer, as a publisher |
| Growth or performance | CAC, new customers | acquisition media | nothing directly, unless incrementality is shown |
| Field or retail marketing | store openings, run-club and event activations | events, product, sponsorship | a per-city launch, a retailer angle |
| Insight or research | studies, tracking | agency budgets | intelligence, if sold as a study |
| CMO | brand health, total budget | everything, once a year | the story, not the tool |

Triggers that actually open a wallet [INFERENCE]: a product launch; entering a new city or opening a store; the incumbent platform's renewal date; a CFO challenge to prove community spend; a run-club or event sponsorship renewal; a new leader wanting a signature initiative.

**What evidence would settle it.** From five conversations, a filled buyer map with role, KPI, line and trigger, and at least two marketers describing what happened the week they signed their last new vendor.

**Cheapest test.** The "last vendor" question (Objection 1) answers most of this. At the workshop: build a one-page buyer map for two archetypes (a footwear or apparel brand with retail distribution; a DTC supplement brand) and choose one role and one trigger for the first offer. Write the first offer for that person by name of role.

### Objection 8. The people Sway finds are the ones I cannot deploy against my KPIs; activating Connectors gives me an event budget, not a result I can report

**In one sentence.** Sway's distinctive advocate, the person who never posts but moves product in a club, does not produce the content, reach or referred revenue that my programme is measured on, so an activation package for Connectors competes with my existing event and field spend rather than adding a reportable outcome.

**Why it matters.** "Everyone can have influence" is the reason Sway claims to reach people the creator platforms cannot (`01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt` point 1). If brands cannot report the value of those people, Sway's activation revenue reverts to the same task-and-reward system the incumbents already sell, and "better than Duel" on features is not credible with two founders (`research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 7; `research/context_audit.md` 5.12).

**What triggered it.** `03_ADVOCATE_VALUE_MODEL.md`: the Trust path needs no followers or content. `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "A runner who never posts may cause half her club to switch shoes." `04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt`: the activation package includes "UGC, reviews, referrals, surveys, testing, events", which is the incumbent's job list; Duel publicly describes "tasks, missions and briefs that are completed to earn points that unlock rewards" (https://www.duel.tech/how-it-works/ambassadors [SNIPPET]). The metrics brands are seen using (`research/duel_customer_evidence.md` 4c) are attributed revenue, EMV, member count, UGC volume, engagement and referred revenue share (Roster benchmark: top quartile "5.58% of brand revenue", baseline "0.88%", https://www.prweb.com/releases/the-top-25-of-dtc-ambassador-programs-refer-5-6-of-revenue--millions-a-year-in-sales-from-existing-customers-302876122.html [VENDOR-PUBLISHED]). None has a column for "verified in-person recommender".

I believe the Connector is real; "88% of consumers said that they trusted recommendations from people they know" (Nielsen 2021, https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2021/beyond-martech-building-trust-with-consumers-and-engaging-where-sentiment-is-high/ [SNIPPET]). But my reporting does not have a place for her, and I already have run clubs and events for recognising people I like [INFERENCE].

**What evidence would settle it.** A marketer naming the KPI that Connector activation moves and how they would report it; and a Phase 6 "prove lift" definition written before the pilot in the brand's own terms (referred revenue, new customers, recommendation frequency, recipient action), not in Sway's.

**Cheapest test.** Before the workshop, a forced choice put to each marketer: "£10K to spend: twenty more posting creators, or fifty verified in-person recommenders in one city with their scan and click history? How would you report the second to your CFO?" At the workshop: define "lift" for Phase 6 in a brand's reporting vocabulary, and strike anything from the activation package that simply re-creates the incumbent's task system.

## 1b. How Sway fits alongside my existing platform, in practice

Three honest scenarios from my chair [INFERENCE throughout]:

- **Replace the incumbent.** No. Public pages show Duel offering tasks, tiers, perks, storefronts, commission payouts with tracked statuses, UGC rights capture, a Shopify app and a Klaviyo integration, plus consultancy and programme management (`research/duel_public_offering.md` section 2 [SNIPPET]). Brandbassador is reviewed for "a wide range of missions" and an app that "has never buffered or crashed" (https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brandbassadoras.brandbassador [SNIPPET]). Two founders will not match that in 2027, and the audit's 5.12 says the same.
- **Run alongside as a second programme.** Painful. Two identities for the same advocate, two reward currencies, two sets of terms, and my team runs both. I would not do it voluntarily.
- **Feed my existing programme.** Sway finds people I do not have; I invite them, with their consent, into the programme I already run; Sway keeps the consumer record and the cross-brand history. This is the only fit I would adopt in year one. Its implication is uncomfortable for the revenue model: the first brand product is an export or invite flow with a per-person or per-cohort price, not a $30K subscription (section 3, option E). One caution: Duel's listing describes a "proprietary Advocate Intelligence Graph" (https://www.g2.com/products/duel/reviews [SNIPPET]; detail [NEEDS SOURCE]), so incumbents could plausibly build discovery from their own programme data. Sway's edge has to be the people outside the brand's programme and CRM, and the in-person record, which no public page of any incumbent describes ([NEEDS SOURCE for absence]).

## 2. What must be true for Sway to work from my chair

1. **Density beats what I already know.** In one city, within 90 days of the Alpha, Sway can show a brand at least N members with its products in their Lockers and M distinct recipients who acted, where N and M exceed what the brand's community team, CRM and post-purchase survey already tell it. N and M are set at the workshop and agreed with at least one real marketer before the pilot.
2. **Events reconcile to orders.** Sway's events can be matched, even manually, to a brand's own order data for a pilot, and the matched purchases show a new-customer share and return rate no worse than the brand's existing referral programme.
3. **Affiliate programmes let Sway in.** Three wellness brands' programmes accept Sway as a publisher under their incentivised-traffic and self-referral rules, at a rate that makes the advocate's earning visible and Sway's share more than a rounding error, or the founders decide EARN is not part of the first proof.
4. **Legal accepts the data arrangement.** One brand's DPO or lawyer accepts a written "who owns what" and a DPA in which Sway holds identity and consent, the brand receives exports for its own programme, and records about recipients who never joined are minimised and time-limited.
5. **There is a named buyer, a line and a trigger.** The first offer is written for one role, fits under that role's no-procurement threshold, is sold by an incorporated company with a DPA and a support contact, and is timed to a real trigger (launch, city, renewal, CFO challenge), not to Sway's readiness.
6. **Activation moves a KPI the buyer already reports.** The activation package changes referred revenue, new customers, recommendation frequency or content volume in the brand's own reporting vocabulary, rather than only a metric Sway invents.
7. **Sway coexists with the incumbent.** The brand-side product works as a feed into the brand's existing programme rather than a rip-and-replace of a well-supported platform, so the first sale does not require the brand to abandon anything.

## 3. Stronger alternatives and reframings

Each keeps the consumer core the source of truth protects (account → My 5 → Locker → public profile/recommendation → Sway Link → QR/Sway Pass → basic interaction tracking) and changes only how a brand first pays. None invents a price; every amount is to be discovered in conversation.

### A. Sell a paid discovery study: "the word-of-mouth audit", one brand, one city, fixed term

- **The alternative.** Instead of an intelligence subscription, sell one brand an eight to twelve week study in one city: who recommends you in person, to whom, what they say (the Locker "reason"), which competitor products sit beside yours in the same Lockers, and what observable action followed. Deliverable: a written report the marketer can forward, a live but simple view, and a consented list of people the brand may invite into its own programme.
- **What it changes.** Product: adds a study protocol, a consent and export flow, and a report template; the consumer product is unchanged. Sales: one buyer (community lead or insight lead), one trigger (launch, new city, annual planning), a price under the no-procurement threshold, and a renewal path into activation. Investor story: paid design partners and first revenue in 2027, framed as "paid pilots that fund the network build", with the honesty that it is services-shaped.
- **What it keeps.** Consumer-first, the Locker, the Pass, honest events, density in one city, and the "we found it" claim, provided the cohort is recruited from the category's communities and not from the brand's customer list.
- **Biggest risk.** The consultancy trap: two founders spend the pilot writing reports; a brand-funded cohort contaminates the "organic" claim if recruitment leans on the brand; the brand learns what it needed from one study and does not renew; and a study is easier for an incumbent or an agency to copy than a network is.

### B. Sell to retailers, run stores and studios, where the in-person purchase actually happens

- **The alternative.** The place a run-club recommendation converts is often a specialist running store, a studio's retail corner or a gym shop. Sell the store, not the brand: staff and club captains are the advocates, the Pass resolves to the store's catalogue, and a code at the till (or a receipt upload) lets a scan be matched to a real in-store purchase, which closes the "where visible" gap that objection 3 exposes.
- **What it changes.** Product: a store-level view, a till code or a simple POS hook, a store Locker; wallet and NFC later. Sales: a store manager or retailer marketing lead, smaller contracts, triggers such as a run-club programme or a store opening; brand money reaches Sway later through retailer co-op budgets. Investor story: a local commerce network with a real purchase in the chain; the closest shipped analogue is single-brand offline referral (Extole's QR on receipts and packaging with offline purchase events, https://docs.extole.com/technical/solutions/extole-solution-guides/retail [SNIPPET]), which Sway would make person-owned and cross-brand.
- **What it keeps.** The IRL Pass as hero, community nodes as acquisition, the attribution chain (now with a purchase), the wellness territory.
- **Biggest risk.** Retail margins and retail technology are thin and old; the store owns the customer and will want the data; Sway drifts towards a retailer loyalty tool, which is a cousin of the "brand-owned loyalty app" the advisor skill forbids; brand logos and brand revenue arrive later than in the current plan. [INFERENCE throughout; no retailer has been approached.]

### C. Sell to community organisers as sponsorship proof

- **The alternative.** Run clubs, studios and event organisers already take brand money and product; Heylo lists "sponsor perks" for paying members and Strava's adidas partnership rewards logged miles with adiClub points (https://www.heylo.com/running-club ; https://www.sgieurope.com/fitness/stravas-bigger-platform-play/122208.article [SNIPPET, THIRD-PARTY]). What a captain cannot do is show a sponsor what the club actually moves. Give the club an aggregated, opt-in view of its members' Lockers and recommendation activity, and let the club sell sponsorship with that evidence; Sway charges the club a small fee or takes a share of sponsorship it helps close.
- **What it changes.** Product: a club-level aggregated view and a sponsor report; the Founding Member Collective becomes club collectives. Sales: captains and studio owners are reachable and have a trigger (sponsorship renewal, season start); the real payer is the brand's field marketing team, reached through the club. Investor story: bottom-up distribution through the nodes the plan already targets.
- **What it keeps.** The density strategy, the consumer product, the Pass, the community acquisition model.
- **Biggest risk.** Clubs have no money and are run by volunteers; it becomes a three-sided market; sponsorship budgets are small and already spoken for; and the whole thing pulls Sway towards "run-club-only", which `00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` rules out. [INFERENCE; no captain has been asked.]

### D. A per-city launch partner: one brand per category co-funds the city launch

- **The alternative.** Before the Alpha, sign one wellness brand per category in the launch city as launch partner: they co-fund Founding Member rewards (product drops, race entries) and get first access to the city's Trust Map and the first Collective, with the partnership clearly labelled in the product.
- **What it changes.** Product: a labelled partner Collective from day one (the source of truth allows "a clearly labeled example Collective"); reward operations partly funded. Sales: a sponsorship-style deal that field marketing already knows how to sign, with a city launch as the trigger and per-city category exclusivity as the hook. Investor story: an early logo and early cash, sold as sponsorship rather than SaaS.
- **What it keeps.** The consumer Locker open to every brand, density in one city, the Pass and the honest event model.
- **Biggest risk.** It contaminates the organic claim: "we found your community" becomes "we built it for our partner"; it cuts against the guardrail "Do not require brand participation for the consumer network to begin"; the partner expects control, exclusivity and results on its timetable; and the partner's competitors will not trust the data later.

### E. A recruitment feed into the brand's existing programme, priced per verified recommender

- **The alternative.** Do not ask the brand to move anything. Sway finds people who demonstrably recommend the brand in person and online, shows a consented profile (products, recipients, observable actions, no exact location), and lets the brand invite them into its existing Duel, Brandbassador or in-house programme. Charge per accepted invitation or per quarterly cohort.
- **What it changes.** Product: an invite and export flow with consent, and a per-person "verified recommender" summary; no Collective needed at first. Sales: the ambassador manager, whose live problem is programme growth and applicant quality (Duel's public "Smart Moderation" exists to filter applicants, https://www.duel.tech/blog/streamline-your-brand-ambassador-program-with-ai-powered-smart-moderation-brand-advocacy-software [SNIPPET]); the trigger is a growth target or a quality complaint. Investor story: low contract value and it looks like lead generation, but it is the fastest path to a paid brand relationship and to proving the data changes a brand decision.
- **What it keeps.** Everything consumer-side; coexistence with incumbents instead of rip-and-replace; the Trust Map as the long-term asset.
- **Biggest risk.** Contract value is small; the brand pays once and then owns the relationship; Sway becomes a funnel for the incumbents; and an incumbent could plausibly build discovery from its own data (see 1b). The mitigation is that Sway's people are, by design, outside any brand's programme.

## 4. Discussion prompts for the workshop, verbatim

Put these to each other exactly as written, ideally with two minutes of silent writing before anyone speaks.

1. "Name the person, by job title, at one wellness brand who would sign Sway's first invoice. Which line of their budget does it come from, and what has to happen in their year for that line to open?"
2. "If a brand said 'send me the list and I will invite them into my existing programme', would we say yes? What would we charge, and what would Sway have left afterwards?"
3. "What is the smallest number of members holding one brand's products in one city that would make a marketer take a meeting, and what number would make them pay? Write both numbers down before we discuss them."
4. "Which of our events, scan, view, save, click, or purchase where visible, could a brand's finance team check against their own order data, and how would they do it in a pilot with no integration?"
5. "If a Sway-attributed sale turns out to be an existing customer buying through a friend's link, what do we tell the brand, and do we still take the commission?"
6. "Would we accept being approved in a brand's affiliate programme at 3 to 5 per cent, with our traffic flagged as friends and family, for all of 2027? If yes, what does EARN look like on the Locker at that rate?"
7. "Who owns the advocate when a brand's contract ends: the brand, Sway, or the person? Write the one paragraph we would put in a contract."
8. "A brand's lawyer asks what we store about a recipient who scanned but never joined, for how long, and on what legal basis. What is our answer today, in plain words?"
9. "Do we sell intelligence, or do we give it away to sell activation? Decide, and say what changes on the pricing slide."
10. "Is a paid, fixed-term word-of-mouth study for one brand in one city a betrayal of consumer-first, or the cheapest way to fund it? Argue each side for five minutes, then decide."
11. "If a running retailer offered to put a code at the till so a Pass scan could be matched to an in-store purchase, would we take it, and what would that do to our brand-side story?"
12. "Which KPI that a community manager already reports, referred revenue, new customers, content pieces or members, does Connector activation move? If we cannot name one, what is the activation package for?"
13. "Using public information only, what is our two-sentence answer to 'the incumbents already describe an advocate intelligence graph, so why is your Trust Map different'?"
14. "Which brand conversations have actually happened, with whom, on what date, and what did they say they would pay for? If the answer is none, which five do we book before Wednesday 7 October?"
15. "What is the largest amount a community manager at a £50M brand can spend without procurement, and does our first offer fit inside it? If we do not know, who finds out this week?"
16. "Our plan reaches brands after density in one city. What is the earliest month a brand invoice could realistically be paid, and can we fund the company until then without one?"
