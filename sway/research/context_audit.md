## 1. ESTABLISHED: decisions and positions the documents present as settled

Everything in this section is [DECIDED] unless marked otherwise. The file each item comes from is given so writers can quote it.

### 1.1 The source-of-truth hierarchy (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`)

1. "The current decisions in `01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` through `05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md`."
2. "The approved narrow MVP boundary and founder decisions recorded in this package."
3. "The attached strategy PDFs as historical strategic context and rationale."
4. "Older expansive ideas as future possibilities only."

"The current strategy reflects founder discussions that occurred after portions of the PDFs were written. Do not silently restore older scope."

### 1.2 The canonical product spine, verbatim (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`)

"Keep this wording and order exactly:"

**MY 5 → LOCKER → SWAY PASS/LINK → RECOMMEND → EARN → TRUST → SWAY FOUNDING MEMBER COLLECTIVE → TRUST MAP → BRAND RECOGNITION → BRAND COLLECTIVE → ACTIVATION → SCALE.**

Note for writers: the spine is twelve steps. The first seven are consumer-side and are the subject of the launch. Steps eight to twelve are the brand-side progression and are later layers. The spine is a narrative order, not an MVP scope statement; the MVP boundary is separate (1.6).

### 1.3 The one thing, the buyer-facing line, and the category (`01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`)

- The one thing: **"Sway turns trusted product recommendations into measurable commerce."**
- Buyer-facing version: **"Sway helps wellness brands turn real customers and community members into measurable advocates."**
- Category description: "Sway is a consumer technology and commerce platform for trusted recommendations. It gives people one place to keep and share the products they genuinely stand behind, then helps measure what happens when friends and communities act on those recommendations. Over time, brands can recognize and activate the real people already creating value for them."

### 1.4 The launch wedge and why (`01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`)

- "Begin with measurable word of mouth for wellness brands, built through dense wellness and healthy-living communities. Communities are where recommendations already happen frequently and credibly; they are the route to consumer density, not the product's permanent boundary."
- The first proof is that people will: "1. Build a useful recommendation identity. 2. Use it naturally when someone asks what they recommend. 3. Generate trackable scans, clicks, saves, and purchases. 4. Return because the Locker, progress, recognition, and rewards are valuable."
- Why this wedge: "Its distinctive opportunity is the missing connective layer: persistent, product-level evidence of who genuinely influences whom across both digital and real-world recommendations."
- Later expansion rights: "brand intelligence, Collectives, creative briefs, reviews, research, community activation, content rights, and enterprise workflows."

### 1.5 The current non-negotiables (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`)

1. "Sway's focused entry point is measurable trusted product recommendations: word of mouth that can be attributed to a person, product, recipient, action, and, where visible, purchase." [punctuation adjusted]
2. "Wellness and healthy living are the launch territory. Dense communities are acquisition nodes. Sway is not a run-club-only product."
3. "The consumer product must be valuable before a brand becomes a customer."
4. "The Locker is the product home. It contains real products and genuine personal reasons."
5. "A Sway Pass is the real-world counterpart of an affiliate link."
6. "One advocate identity can create value through Reach, Creation, and Trust. Creator, Innovator, and Connector are capability signals, not separate classes of people."
7. "Brand Collectives are branded environments inside Sway, not separate ecosystems. They allow a brand to activate, grow, and measure its own advocate community."
8. "Brand pricing tiers describe what a brand buys from Sway. They are not advocate achievement tiers."

### 1.6 The immediate MVP boundary (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`)

"The durable core is:"

**account → My 5 → Locker → public profile/recommendation → Sway Link → QR/Sway Pass → basic interaction tracking**

"The Alpha/Beta product may test lightweight points, rewards, attribution feedback, and a clearly labeled example Collective, but it must not pretend advanced infrastructure or brand relationships already exist."

"Preserve as later layers: scaled Creator/Innovator workflows, private creative briefs, reviews programs, research/user testing, sophisticated gamification, content licensing, advanced segmentation, AI, enterprise integrations, native wallet sophistication, and fully managed/white-glove brand services."

The historical PDF (`03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt`) has a longer "do not build yet" list that remains consistent with this: "Full Brand Command Center • complex AI segmentation • sophisticated gamification • full Content Studio • enterprise integrations • multi-market admin • advanced licensing • giant creator marketplace." And `03_ADVOCATE_VALUE_MODEL.md`: "Do not build a full creative-production marketplace into the consumer MVP."

### 1.7 The strategic guardrails (`01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`)

- "Do not lead with UGC, reviews, or AI visibility. They are valuable outputs and later modules, not the initial wedge."
- "Do not define advocacy by follower count. Public reach is one form of value."
- "Do not make the experience feel like couponing or a transactional affiliate utility."
- "Do not require brand participation for the consumer network to begin. Use available affiliate infrastructure where appropriate, while clearly labeling eligibility and pending/confirmed/reversed outcomes."
- "Do not claim that attribution is perfect. Capture observable events and state what remains unknown."

`04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md` adds a selling guardrail: "Do not lead with a universal platform promising UGC, reviews, research, loyalty, affiliate commerce, influencer management, events, AI visibility, and enterprise orchestration at once."

`SKILL_sway_advisor.md` adds: "Do not turn Sway into a run-club-only product, a generic influencer platform, a UGC marketplace, or a brand-owned loyalty app."

### 1.8 The language discipline (`00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`)

- "'Trust' is evidence that people act because of a recommendation; it is not merely activity points."
- "'Sway Points' reward eligible participation and create status progression. They must not be presented as identical to Trust."
- "'Collective tiers' are advocate progression levels within one brand program."
- "'Brand service tiers' or 'brand packages' are commercial packages a brand purchases from Sway."
- "Do not use 'Gold tier' without stating which system is meant. Prefer 'highest brand service package' for white-glove service."

### 1.9 The long-term moat as stated (`01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`)

"The defensible asset is a growing graph of human influence: people, products, recommendations, recipients, communities, actions, purchases, geography, time, and repeat behavior. Each credible event makes the Trust Map more useful. The QR code, affiliate link, and wallet pass are mechanisms; the persistent history of trusted influence is the strategic asset."

This is a founder position on what the moat should be. Whether it is a moat is a hypothesis (see 2.11 and 4.4).

### 1.10 Product model positions (`02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md`)

- One identity, one foundation: "One Sway identity. One master Locker containing products across brands. One personal Sway Pass for broad real-world recommendation moments. Product-specific recommendation pages and affiliate-capable Sway Links."
- "The member should not need a different personal QR code for every product. A personal Pass begins a short recommendation session; the advocate selects the product from the Locker, optionally edits the reason, and Sway creates the attributable recommendation. A product page can also expose a direct product-specific QR or link when context makes that faster."
- Core attribution chain: **"Advocate → product → recommendation → recipient → action → purchase, where visible"**. "Capture brand, product, channel, community/context, timestamp, approximate geography when consented, and repeat behavior. Preserve unknowns. Do not infer a purchase from a click or a trusted relationship from raw activity alone."
- Two surfaces, one engine: "Digital: a trackable Sway Link, including affiliate parameters where eligible. Real world: a unique Sway Pass or recommendation QR that resolves through Sway before the recipient acts."
- Advocate journey (seven steps, "Tori"): clean product-forward Locker; add by search, URL or manual entry with optional reason (scanning the physical product "must not be required"); product image as primary visual; one action begins a recommendation, one action edits it; "View My Sway Pass" with "a unique, identifiable QR and an eventual Add to Apple Wallet path"; recipient scans and the advocate selects the product or it is already encoded; advocate "sees observable outcomes and any eligible pending/confirmed/reversed affiliate earnings."
- Recipient journey ("Blake"): a "fast, branded, mobile recommendation page" showing the recognisable product, who recommends it, the short personal reason, "the recipient benefit, if one genuinely exists", and "one dominant next action." Progression: "1. Continue to the product or claim the available benefit. 2. Optionally save the product using email. 3. Optionally join/download Sway to keep scans, earn eligible Sway Points, and build a Locker." "Do not force an app download before the recipient understands the recommendation and value. Do not promise a discount unless affiliate or brand terms actually provide one."
- Locker: "products the member stands behind; the action center for adding, editing, sharing, and recommending."
- Trust: "evidence of influence. Include two simple views: Real World and Affiliate." Real World is "an actual geographic map using approximate city-level influence"; Affiliate shows "clicks, attributed purchases, sales, commission, conversion rate, and pending/confirmed/reversed status."
- Progress: "Sway Points, overall status, next threshold, rewards, attributed outcomes, and a high-level view of Collective participation. Use an airline-status-style visual."
- Brand Collectives: "A brand does not receive a separate Sway ecosystem. It activates, grows, and measures a distinct branded community inside Sway." Example structure (HOKA): Home, Challenges, Tiers & Rewards, HOKA Locker. "A Collective can show ten advocate achievement tiers, locked or unlocked by brand-specific points earned through challenges. These are separate from Sway-wide status and from the commercial package the brand purchases." (This is a later-layer specification; see 3.9.)

### 1.11 Advocate value model positions (`03_ADVOCATE_VALUE_MODEL.md`)

- "Sway helps brands activate people for three kinds of value: Reach, Creation, and Trust." "These are contribution paths, not permanent identity boxes."
- Reach = public influence (social content, affiliate links, traffic, purchases); capability signal **Creator**.
- Creation = "valuable assets for a brand without needing an audience or social posting" (private briefs, polished photos or video, licensing); capability signal **Innovator**.
- Trust = "Influence real people through credibility, relationships, and community" (attributable IRL recommendations, direct links, reviews, testing, research feedback, events, referrals, community activity); capability signal **Connector**.
- "Reviews, research, events, and recommendations are activities, not additional advocate identities."
- "The Vidsy lesson" (creative ability can be separated from audience size) is explicitly a later Innovator workflow.

### 1.12 Commercial and launch positions (`04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md`)

- Launch sequence: "Start with consumers, not brand contracts." Six steps: recruit a small set of Founding Members in dense wellness communities in one city; test My 5, Locker creation, digital sharing, IRL Sway Pass behaviour, recipient action, repeat use; build density not national spread; reveal patterns through the Trust Map; approach brands with proof; activate the first Brand Collective and measure lift.
- The brand pitch: **"Your community already exists. We found it."** (also `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` Phase 4).
- Founding Member value: "Before brands participate, Sway Points should create visible progress and unlock Sway-funded access or rewards. Trust must be earned through evidence that people value a recommendation. Points and Trust are related but distinct." Potential rewards: "classes, recovery sessions, race entries, product drops, experiences, founding-member recognition, testing, and local perks. Validate what members value; avoid a discount-only feeling."
- Revenue architecture, four layers: Commerce (affiliate commission shared with the advocate), Intelligence, Activation, Scale.
- On the numbers: "The draft model proposes an advocate-friendly 80/20 split... It also contains illustrative brand packages of $30K, $75K, and $150K+ annually and proposed transaction fees for content and rewards. **These are planning assumptions to validate, not contracted prices, forecasts, or settled policy.**" [punctuation adjusted]
- Commercial principle: "Silver/activation must be capable of producing a successful advocacy program. A higher brand service package exists to scale a proven program and may include white-glove management outside the app."
- Metrics for the first proof (the agreed measurement list): first product and My 5 completion; Locker quality and repeat additions; recommendation shares and unique IRL scans; recipient product views, saves, outbound clicks, attributable purchases where available; repeat recommendation behaviour; recipient-to-member conversion; brand/product/community density; retention and evidence that members care about progress, Trust or rewards.

## 2. HYPOTHESES: claims presented as true that are untested

Every item below is a [HYPOTHESIS]. Evidence column is what the package contains; in every case it is reasoning and analogy, not observed behaviour, member data, or brand conversations. The "cheapest test" is what can be done before or during the four weeks around the workshop, by two people, with no engineering. Question banks already drafted in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` sections 6a and 6b, and the hands-on alternative tests in `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 6, are referenced rather than repeated.

### 2.1 People will build a Locker because it is useful to them, starting from "My 5"

- Why it matters: it is step one of the spine and the entire cold start. `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` states the MVP's single question: "will someone build a Sway because it is useful to them, then naturally use it when another person asks what they recommend?"
- Evidence in the package: none. The "five things worth the money" concept is a design idea. The PDF's Week 1 plan to "Interview 30-50 people using the '5 things worth the money' concept" is a plan, not a result.
- Cheapest test: ask ten people in a wellness community to answer "what are the five things actually worth the money?" in a notes app or a paper card, with a reason each. Time how long it takes, count who finishes five, note which products appear, and whether they ask to keep or share the list. Two evenings, one founder, before Friday 2 October.

### 2.2 Advocates will use their Sway naturally when someone asks what they recommend ("Scan my Sway", "It's in my Sway")

- Why it matters: the behaviour the whole product depends on; `03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt` calls it "the behavior we want to create."
- Evidence: none. It is a slogan.
- Cheapest test: a "Wizard of Oz" pass. Give five people a link-in-bio page with their five products and a printed QR (any free link tool; see `research/alternatives_landscape.md` Test 5). Ask them to use it for one week when a recommendation moment comes up and keep a tally of moments, uses, and reasons for not using it. Cost near zero; one week; must start by Friday 25 September to report before the workshop.

### 2.3 Recipients will scan a personal QR in a real social moment, and the scan will resolve into an action

- Why it matters: the Sway Pass is the "real-world counterpart of an affiliate link" (non-negotiable 5) and the differentiator against link-only platforms. If the scan moment is socially awkward or too slow, the IRL hero collapses to a digital link product.
- Evidence: none observed. `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 4 found brand-scoped QR referral mechanics (Extole, impact.com/advocate, Talkable Wallet) and no consumer-owned cross-brand pass; so the mechanic exists in adjacent contexts, but recipient behaviour toward a personal pass is unobserved [SNIPPET].
- Cheapest test: the same five-person Wizard of Oz week (2.2), recording the recipient side: did the other person scan, save, ask for the link instead, or decline. Blake and Tory should each also try to be recipients twice. Also run the ten "intercept" questions (`duel_customer_evidence.md` 6b, question 7: "Would you show a QR or link on your phone to a friend who asked... What would stop you?").

### 2.4 Recipients will act on a recommendation page (view, save with email, click out, buy where visible)

- Why it matters: it is the "action" in the attribution chain and the basis of any Trust or earnings.
- Evidence: none. The recipient page design is reasoned from good practice.
- Cheapest test: build the recipient page first in the workshop prototype (it is one screen) and put it in front of five strangers in Barcelona or before the trip; watch what they tap first. Pre-workshop: use any free landing page tool with a real product, a real reason and a real "continue" link and watch three people use it.

### 2.5 Recipients will convert to members (recipient-to-member conversion)

- Why it matters: it is the only organic growth loop described; without it, acquisition depends entirely on community outreach.
- Evidence: none.
- Cheapest test: cannot be tested honestly before a product exists. Record as the key Alpha metric and define the threshold at the workshop (05_FOUNDER: "What counts as success, failure, or a pivot after the first cohort?").

### 2.6 Members will return because of "the Locker, progress, recognition, and rewards" (retention before brands)

- Why it matters: non-negotiable 3 ("valuable before a brand becomes a customer") stands or falls on it. `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt`: "Sway is not meant to be a one-off affiliate utility."
- Evidence: none. The retention drivers are listed, not tested.
- Cheapest test: in the Wizard of Oz week, ask at day seven "would you open this again next week, and for what?" (6b question 8). Also ask what they would want in return for recommending (6b question 5) and what they would never want.

### 2.7 Recognition ("HOKA notices") is a motivator strong enough to sustain participation for people who are not creators

- Why it matters: it is the "second incentive beyond money" in `02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.txt` and the consumer payoff of the whole brand-side progression ("YOU'VE BEEN INVITED TO THE HOKA COLLECTIVE").
- Evidence: none. And the payoff cannot exist until a brand pays for a Collective, which is months away under the launch sequence.
- Cheapest test: in intercepts, offer a forced choice between three rewards of equal Sway cost (cash-equivalent commission, a local experience, a named recognition from a brand they love) and ask them to rank. Ten people. Also ask former ambassadors what made them stop (6b question 3).

### 2.8 Sway-funded Founding Member rewards (classes, recovery sessions, race entries, drops, merchandise) are valued, affordable, and do not feel like couponing

- Why it matters: the Founding Member Collective is the stated bridge across the cold start ("Sway itself becomes the first Collective").
- Evidence: none. There is no budget anywhere in the package (see 6.2), and no reward has been priced.
- Cheapest test: price five candidate rewards with two local providers (a studio, a run club, a recovery space) and ask whether they would co-fund or gift them in exchange for members. One founder, three phone calls, before the workshop. Ask intercept participants which of the five they would actually turn up for.

### 2.9 Dense wellness communities are efficient acquisition nodes and community leaders will grant access

- Why it matters: "Broad category. Narrow communities." is the acquisition model; paid media is ruled out ("Do not target 'wellness consumers' broadly with paid media").
- Evidence: none. The six node types (run clubs, Pilates and yoga, boutique fitness and strength, cycling and training groups, recovery and wellness spaces, healthy-living events) are reasoned, not measured. No community leader has been contacted according to the package.
- Cheapest test: contact three community leaders in the candidate city this month with the one-line pitch ("Tell us the 5 things actually worth the money. We'll turn them into your Sway."). Record who replies, what they ask for, and whether they would host a session. Owner: whichever founder lives in the candidate city.

### 2.10 Density in one city will produce visible person-to-person propagation ("surprising" Trust Map patterns) within a pilot cohort

- Why it matters: Phase 3 and Phase 4 of the PDF plan and step 4 of the current launch sequence depend on the data becoming "surprising" enough to "take the meeting."
- Evidence: none. This is also a maths question: how many scan events per member per month, across how many members, for how long, before any pattern is more than noise. The package does not attempt the estimate.
- Cheapest test: at the workshop, define what "surprising" means in numbers (for example, "at least N distinct products each recommended by at least M members to at least K distinct recipients in one community"). Then estimate whether a 100-member cohort could reach it in 90 days at plausible event rates. If not, the brand-approach date moves.

### 2.11 The Trust Map is a moat: it compounds, it is hard to copy, and it is more valuable than what ShopMy, LTK, impact.com or Duel already hold

- Why it matters: it is the stated long-term defensible asset (1.9) and the investor story.
- Evidence: none beyond argument. `01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt` itself concedes "Sway cannot differentiate on the word trust alone" because ShopMy's Trusted By already uses saves, Circle participation and purchases [SNIPPET, confirmed in `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 7]. Duel publicly describes a "proprietary Advocate Intelligence Graph" [SNIPPET, G2 listing via `research/duel_public_offering.md` section 6], so the "graph of influence" language is not unique.
- Cheapest test: a thought experiment at the workshop, written down: what data would Sway hold after 90 days that none of those four could get, and which brand decision would change because of it. If no brand decision changes, the Trust Map is a feature, not a moat, in the wedge phase.

### 2.12 Brands will pay for intelligence about organic advocacy (the "see it" package) before any activation

- Why it matters: it is the first brand revenue layer and the "Bronze" tier of the PDFs; $30K per year is the illustrative price.
- Evidence: none. Duel does not publish pricing [SNIPPET, https://www.affililist.com/software/duel] and no comparable "intelligence only" product with a published price was found in the sibling research.
- Cheapest test: the three to five wellness-brand marketer conversations already scoped in `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` Gap 3, adding: "If we could show you the people already recommending you in person in one city, what would you do with that, and what is it worth?" Do not quote a price.

### 2.13 Brands will pay for activation (the "activate it" package) and will fund boosted commissions of 16 to 18 per cent for high-Trust advocates

- Why it matters: activation is the main recurring revenue layer; the "Collective target" economics make the advocate proposition "financially meaningful."
- Evidence: none for wellness. The 16 to 20 per cent band comes from LTK's guidance to brands about creators [SNIPPET, https://onbrand.shopltk.com/how-to-choose-the-right-commission-rate-for-influencers]. `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 5: "no wellness brand was found publicly paying it to non-creators."
- Cheapest test: in the same brand conversations, ask what they currently pay ambassadors and affiliates, on what basis, and whether they have ever paid more for a demonstrably higher-converting person.

### 2.14 Affiliate coverage in wellness is sufficient for a pilot and rates make "Earn" meaningful

- Why it matters: EARN is step five of the spine; the guardrail says use "available affiliate infrastructure" and the Executive Summary claims "Eligible recommendations generate affiliate revenue from day one."
- Evidence in the package: none. The PDF worked example assumes "Standard affiliate rate 10% = $10" [ILLUSTRATIVE]. External evidence gathered today points the other way for the launch category: HOKA's own programme page reported as 5 per cent full price and 3 per cent sale [SNIPPET, https://www.hoka.com/en/us/hoka-affiliate-program.html]; Amazon Associates Health and Personal Care reported at 1 per cent by most 2026 guides [UNVERIFIED third-party, official schedule blocked]; lululemon about 5 to 10 per cent [third-party]; supplements 8 to 12 per cent direct [third-party directories]. A $100 HOKA purchase at 5 per cent gives $4 to the advocate under 80/20. Amazon Associates also closes applications without three qualifying sales within 180 days [SNIPPET, per `research/prototype_feasibility.md` 4.2].
- Cheapest test: take the actual products named by the ten My 5 respondents (2.1) and check each against Awin, impact.com, Rakuten, Amazon and the brand's own site for a UK programme, its rate, and its approval requirements. Two hours, one founder, spreadsheet. This turns a hypothesis into a coverage percentage before the workshop.

### 2.15 Sway can monetise "from day one" through affiliate commission while the network grows

- Why it matters: it is a stated investor point ("Sway can monetize before a brand becomes a direct customer").
- Evidence: none. Depends on 2.14, on network approvals (days to weeks), and on volume; with small cohorts and low wellness rates the revenue would be immaterial.
- Cheapest test: the 2.14 spreadsheet plus a one-line arithmetic: cohort size × recommendations per member per month × click-through × conversion × basket × rate × 20 per cent. Present it at the workshop with every input labelled as a guess. It will show whether "day one revenue" belongs in the deck.

### 2.16 The 80/20 advocate split is attractive and workable

- Why it matters: it is the constant in the revenue model and the "advocate-friendly" claim.
- Evidence: benchmark only. ShopMy discloses 82/18 [SNIPPET, https://guide.shopmy.us/289138ecba514337965ea05d1896d1e7]. Whether 80/20 is workable after network fees (Awin describes a network fee as an override percentage but publishes no rate [SNIPPET, https://www.awin.com/us/faqs]), payout costs and reversals is unknown.

## 4. THE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL ASSUMPTIONS, ranked by impact if wrong × uncertainty

1. **People will present, and others will scan, a personal QR in a real social moment.** Impact: the Sway Pass is the mechanism that distinguishes Sway from link-in-bio and creator storefronts; the "real-world counterpart of an affiliate link" is the founders' most specific claim to novelty, and `research/alternatives_landscape.md` section 4 confirms brand-scoped QR referral already exists, so only the person-to-person consumer-owned version is unclaimed. Uncertainty: nothing in the package observes a single such moment, and the social friction (pulling out a phone to be scanned mid-conversation; asking a friend to scan rather than just telling them the product) is real. If wrong, the product collapses to a trackable link with a Locker, competing directly with ShopMy, LTK, Beacons and Linktree on their ground. The Wizard of Oz week (2.2, 2.3) is the cheapest way to learn something before Barcelona.

2. **A consumer product with no brand participation will retain members long enough for anything else to happen.** Impact: every step of the spine after LOCKER, the entire launch sequence, and the investor story ("Sway creates the demand before it sells the infrastructure") depend on it. Uncertainty: the retention drivers (Locker, progress, recognition, rewards) are listed, not tested; two of them (recognition by brands, brand-funded rewards) cannot exist in the first months by the plan's own sequencing; the third (Sway-funded rewards) has no budget. If wrong, Sway must either sell to brands first (reversing non-negotiable 3) or accept a long, self-funded consumer build.

3. **Affiliate coverage and commission rates in wellness make EARN meaningful, and can be switched on for a pilot.** Impact: EARN is in the spine, "Earnings" is in the Locker, "Affiliate" is one of two Trust views, the 80/20 split is the model's constant, and "monetise from day one" is an investor point. Uncertainty: the package's worked example uses 10 per cent [ILLUSTRATIVE]; today's snippet-level evidence puts HOKA at 3 to 5 per cent and Amazon health at about 1 per cent, and network and brand approvals take days to weeks. If wrong, EARN becomes a token line ("a few pence per recommendation"), the "not couponing" guardrail is strained by tiny commissions, and the deck's day-one revenue claim must go. This can be turned into a number before the workshop (2.14).

4. **Brands will pay for intelligence about organic advocacy before, and separately from, activation, and the Trust Map will be "surprising" enough to earn the meeting.** Impact: this is the first brand revenue and the hinge between the consumer phase and the business. Uncertainty: no brand conversation is recorded; `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` finds that brands in this category struggle to prove value to finance and value owning their data, which cuts both ways; and 2.10 shows nobody has estimated how much activity a cohort needs before any pattern is real. If wrong, Sway's first brand revenue is activation, which requires a product the MVP boundary excludes.

5. **Dense communities in one city are reachable, their leaders will cooperate, and density produces person-to-person propagation rather than a scattering of isolated Lockers.** Impact: the acquisition model rules out paid media and rests on nodes; the Trust Map's value rests on propagation, not on individual profiles. Uncertainty: no community leader has been approached; the founders' own community memberships are not stated; whether either founder lives in the launch city is unknown. If wrong, cohort recruitment is slow and the data never clusters.

6. **The two founders can execute the plan at the required pace alongside their current commitments.** Impact: total. The 90-day plan implies community recruitment, interviews, prototype iteration, affiliate set-up, reward operations and brand outreach, with no engineer, no budget line and no stated weekly hours. Uncertainty: the package is silent on time commitment, employment constraints, funding and incorporation (section 6). If wrong, every date in every plan slips, and investors will ask the question first.

7. **Recognition and status, rather than money, will motivate non-creators to keep recommending.** Impact: it is the reason Sway claims it can reach people ShopMy and LTK cannot (no follower gate), the point of Points and the airline-status Progress view, and the emotional payoff ("HOKA notices"). Uncertainty: untested, and structurally delayed until a brand signs. If wrong, Sway's advocates are the same creators everyone else already serves, and the "everyone can have influence" positioning has no user behind it.

8. **Observable, honestly labelled attribution events are both achievable (technically and legally) and persuasive.** Impact: the honesty guardrail is the basis of the differentiation from composite metrics, and the attribution chain is the product. Uncertainty: the recipient must be identified (email save, sign-up, or device signal) for person-to-recipient attribution to exist at all; approximate geography needs consent; UK data protection and affiliate disclosure obligations are unexamined in the package [NEEDS SOURCE]; and whether a finance person prefers a sparse honest event list to a large composite number is unproven. If wrong, the map is thin, the chain breaks at "recipient", and the honesty becomes a weakness in sales.

Ranked just below the eight: the Trust Map as a moat rather than a feature (2.11), and the "why now" claims that have no source (2.20). Both matter for the deck more than for the pilot.

## 5. STRATEGIC TENSIONS THE WORKSHOP MUST RESOLVE

Each is "X versus Y." The current documents lean one way in most cases (noted), but the lean is a position, not a tested outcome. Implications are given for product, sales and fundraising.

### 5.1 Consumer-first versus brand-first revenue timing

- Current lean: consumer-first (non-negotiable 3; "Start with consumers, not brand contracts").
- Consumer-first implies: product = Locker, Pass, Link, recipient page, tracking; no brand dashboard. Sales = none for months; Tory's role becomes community recruitment and brand discovery conversations without a product to sell. Fundraising = a consumer-network pre-seed story with usage metrics as the milestone and revenue deferred; investors will ask how long the runway is before brand revenue.
- Brand-first implies: product = a lightweight brand view over the same data and an activation tool sooner; sales = a design-partner brand in wellness paying a nominal fee; fundraising = a B2B story with an early logo but a weaker "we found the community" proof, and direct comparison with Duel and Brandbassador from day one.
- Middle path to consider: consumer-first build, with two or three wellness brands as unpaid observers who see anonymised patterns in exchange for feedback and a right of first refusal on the first Collective.

### 5.2 IRL Sway Pass versus digital Sway Link as the hero

- Current lean: both, one engine; the Pass gets the emotional weight ("Scan my Sway").
- Pass-as-hero implies: product = the Pass screen, a fast recipient page, a wallet path later, and scanning tested in person; the prototype must demonstrate a scan. Sales = the story brands cannot get elsewhere (in-person attribution). Fundraising = a novel mechanic, but one the room will immediately try and judge; if the demo scan is clunky, it costs credibility.
- Link-as-hero implies: product = a beautiful shareable page and Locker that works in WhatsApp and group chats today; the Pass is a QR of the same link. Sales = easier proof, but the comparison set becomes ShopMy, LTK, Beacons, Linktree. Fundraising = a crowded category unless the Trust and recipient-side data are the story.
- Note: the prototype plan (`research/prototype_feasibility.md`) already builds one route that serves both, so the tension is about narrative emphasis and what gets tested first, not architecture.

### 5.3 Wellness communities versus any category

- Current lean: wellness territory, dense communities, "not a run-club-only product", and My 5 may contain anything.
- Wellness-only implies: product copy, product database seeding and reward calendar are wellness-specific; sales = wellness brands (supplements, footwear, apparel, recovery, sleep, skincare-as-wellness); fundraising = a clear beachhead but lower affiliate rates than beauty and a smaller initial TAM story.
- Any-category implies: a broader consumer product, harder community targeting, no obvious first brand set, and a "what is it for" problem in the deck.
- The unresolved sub-question: does the launch accept that many My 5 products will be outside wellness (headphones, luggage) and therefore outside the brand set Sway wants to prove?

### 5.4 Affiliate economics versus recognition as the advocate motivator

- Current lean: both, with Points and Trust as the recognition system and affiliate share as money; "avoid a discount-only feeling."
- Economics-first implies: product = earnings front and centre, real affiliate links from Alpha, payout operations; sales = commission-led brand conversations; fundraising = a take-rate model investors understand, but small numbers at wellness rates (assumption 3) and a "just another affiliate app" objection.
- Recognition-first implies: product = Progress, Trust map, Founding Member rewards, and earnings shown quietly or later; sales = brand recognition and access as the currency; fundraising = a community-and-data story with revenue further out.
- Recognition-first is more consistent with the guardrails; economics-first is easier to demonstrate and to explain. The workshop should decide what is on the Locker's first screen.

### 5.5 Trust Map as data moat versus Sway as a simple, useful tool

- Current lean: the map is the moat; the tool is how the map is built.
- Moat-first implies: product = consent, identity capture on the recipient side, geography, event schema designed for graph analysis from day one; sales = intelligence packages; fundraising = a data-network story with a long build before defensibility appears, and questions about privacy and whether ShopMy's Trusted By or Duel's graph already occupy the space.
- Tool-first implies: product = the best Locker and recipient page on the market, minimal data collection; sales = per-brand activation when it comes; fundraising = a product story that must explain why it wins against incumbents on experience alone.
- The honest framing for the deck: the tool is the wedge; the map is the ambition; the pilot proves the first, not the second.

### 5.6 London versus another city

- Current lean: none stated. `02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md` uses "Blake in London" as an example only. Where each founder lives, and therefore who can attend a weekly run club in the pilot city, is not in the package.
- London implies: dense communities and events, high cost of rewards, strong competition for attention, the incumbent's home city (Duel is London-headquartered [SNIPPET, https://www.duel.tech/about-us]), and UK affiliate programmes and UK data and advertising rules apply.
- Another UK city (for example where the other founder lives) implies: easier to reach density in a smaller pool, fewer distractions, cheaper rewards, less relevant as an investor showcase. A non-UK city (Barcelona is only the workshop venue) implies a language and affiliate-coverage problem and is not supported by anything in the package.
- Decision rule suggestion: the pilot city is wherever the founder who owns pilot recruitment can be physically present twice a week.

### 5.7 Sway Points versus Trust: two currencies or one

- Current lean: two, deliberately different.
- Two currencies implies: product = a Progress view (Points, status, thresholds, rewards) and a Trust view (map and affiliate outcomes), each needing explanation; sales = Trust is what brands buy, Points are what members chase; fundraising = a sophisticated model that may read as over-designed for an Alpha.
- One currency implies: product = a single "influence" number that mixes participation and outcomes, simpler to explain but exactly the "activity points presented as trust" the discipline forbids.
- The workshop must define which events move which currency (3.11), and whether Points appear in the Alpha at all.

### 5.8 Building a network versus building a tool

- Current lean: a network, seeded by a tool.
- Network implies: recipient-to-member conversion is the metric; the recipient page must recruit; density in one city is essential; growth is measured in members and edges; fundraising is a network story with cold-start risk that investors price in.
- Tool implies: any single member gets value alone; growth can be diffuse; the metric is active Lockers and shares; fundraising is a product story without network economics.
- The stated first proof ("Return because the Locker... [is] valuable") is a tool test; "recipient-to-member conversion" is a network test. Both are on the metrics list; the workshop should say which one decides go or no-go after the first cohort.

### 5.9 How much of the vision to show investors versus the wedge

- Current lean: the deck must "argue before polish" and not invent amounts; the PDFs contain the full vision (Collectives, Content Studio, Gold ecosystem, $540K per brand scenarios).
- Vision-heavy implies: a large story (trust infrastructure, four revenue layers, flywheel) with nothing demonstrable behind layers two to four, inviting "this is Duel plus a consumer app" and "where is the evidence" objections.
- Wedge-heavy implies: a small, demonstrable story (a Locker, a Pass, a scan, an honest event) with the vision as one slide; risks reading as a feature, not a company.
- The programme's deck draft should put the wedge as the demonstration and the vision as the reason the wedge matters, with every evidence gap labelled.

### 5.10 Personal Sway Pass versus product-specific QR

- Current lean: both (1.10). One personal Pass that starts a session, plus optional product QRs.
- Personal-Pass-first implies: a two-step scan (scan, then the advocate picks the product), a session concept, and a simpler wallet story; product-QR-first implies: instant resolution to one product, more codes, a stronger link to the Locker card. The prototype can only demonstrate one convincingly in the time available; choose before scope lock.

### 5.11 Founding Member Collective run by hand versus built in product

- Current lean: unclear (3.15). Hand-run implies: a WhatsApp group, a reward calendar in a spreadsheet, personal messages from the founders, no product cost; built implies: Points, tiers and rewards in the Alpha, which the source of truth allows only as "lightweight" and "clearly labeled."

### 5.12 "Better than Duel" on features versus a different job

- Current lean: different job (consumer-owned identity, no follower gate, in-person observation, nobody pays first). `research/duel_customer_evidence.md` section 7: "'Better than Duel' is not credible on features."

### 5.13 Sway-owned advocate relationship versus brand-owned Collective

- Current lean: the identity and attribution stay with Sway; the Collective is the brand's community inside Sway.
- Implications: product = data-sharing and export design for brands; sales = the ownership objection is the first one Tory will hear (2.19); fundraising = the network is more valuable if Sway owns the graph, and less sellable to brands if they cannot own their members. The workshop should write the one-paragraph answer to "who owns the advocate?"

### 5.14 Real affiliate rails in the Alpha versus mocked eligibility

### 5.15 Prototype as stimulus versus prototype as evidence

- Current lean: stimulus (05_FOUNDER; brief). The tension is behavioural: once a working prototype exists, both founders will be tempted to treat a good demo as validation. The programme should fix the rule in the runbook: the prototype demonstrates the journey; only the cohort tests demonstrate demand.

### 5.16 US-dollar, US-centred framing versus a UK first market

## 10. One-paragraph summary for downstream writers

The founders have decided a lot: the spine, the one thing, the wellness-community wedge, a seven-element MVP core, eight non-negotiables, five guardrails, a language discipline, and a consumer-first sequence in which nobody pays until the consumer product works. What they have not done is observe a single member, recipient, community leader or brand; every behavioural, commercial and market claim in the package is a hypothesis or an illustrative number, and the two things the product most depends on (people scanning a personal QR in a social moment, and members returning without any brand present) are the least examined. The PDFs are older and broader than the current strategy and disagree with it on advocate classes, tier names, pass names, member numbers and the 90-day plan; the current documents govern, and this file records the house style to enforce. The workshop's job is not to restore the vision but to choose a city, a cohort size, a hero mechanic, an honest attribution vocabulary, a first-90-days test with numbers, and a division of labour between two people whose available hours, employment constraints, budget and company status the package never states.
