# Alternatives landscape: what a customer could choose instead of Sway

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Sway founders' workshop (Barcelona, Sat 10 and Sun 11 October 2026).
Scope: public sources only. No internal Duel material was used. Every external fact carries a URL and an evidence label.

## How to read this file

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **Snippet**: the fact comes from a web-search result snippet against the named URL. The primary page could not be fetched because the research environment's egress proxy blocked the domain (full list in section 8). Treat as indicative of page content on the crawl date, not as a verified copy.
- **Fetched**: the primary page was fetched and read. Only one page in this file qualifies (developer.apple.com).
- **Third-party**: a review site, blog or data vendor's figure, not the vendor's own page. Always weaker than Snippet from the vendor's domain.
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: could not be confirmed against any vendor page or reputable secondary source.
- **[NEEDS SOURCE]**: a claim that the founders should confirm directly before it goes into external material.

Style: Australian English, no em dashes. Ranges are written "5 to 10%". Currency symbols are as the source states them.

What Sway proposes, restated so the tables have a fixed reference point (from 01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md and 02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md): one consumer identity; a cross-brand Locker of real products with personal reasons; a Sway Link for digital sharing and a personal Sway Pass (QR, eventual Apple Wallet) for in-person moments; attribution of advocate to product to recommendation to recipient to action to purchase where visible; a Trust Map of who influences whom; brands recognising and activating those people later through Brand Collectives; consumer value before any brand pays.

## 3. Doing nothing, described honestly

### The wellness advocate without any tool

What she actually does today, based on the behaviours described in the dark-social sources above and ordinary observation (no primary study was found that measures wellness specifically; treat the pattern as a hypothesis to test in the founders' own interviews):

- Someone at the club asks "what shoes are those?" She answers out loud. If they want more, she says "I'll send you the link", and later pastes the brand URL, an Amazon link, or a screenshot into WhatsApp.
- She keeps favourites in her head, in Instagram saves, in Amazon lists, in a Pinterest board, or in a Notes list titled something like "stuff I recommend". Some people keep nothing and rely on memory.
- If a brand has already given her a referral code (a "give £10 get £10" program run on Mention Me, Yotpo or similar), she may say "use my code", but only for that brand and only if she remembers it.
- She receives nothing except social credit, and she is usually fine with that. Recommending to a friend is a favour, not a job.

Why this is often fine, and why it is Sway's real competitor:

- Zero friction on both sides. The recipient gets the answer in seconds. There is no sign-up, no scan, no disclosure line.
- Trust is intrinsic. The recommendation is credible because nothing is in it for her. Any visible commission, points or "pass" changes the social meaning of the act. Whether that change is acceptable in a run club is unknown and must be tested, not assumed.
- Most people who influence a small circle do not want an audience, a storefront, or a payout ledger. The Sway PDFs argue this makes them valuable; it also makes them hard to activate.
- The evidence that the 61% (HubSpot via paxcom.ai) and 84% (RadiumOne 2016) private-sharing figures apply to wellness in 2026 is thin and dated. Do not cite them as market sizing.

What "doing nothing" cannot do, which is the space Sway is trying to occupy: keep a durable, product-level record with a reason; let a friend act on it later without asking again; show the advocate any outcome; let a brand ever know she exists.

### The wellness brand without Sway

What a brand of HOKA's or a mid-size supplement company's size typically does today (assembled from the vendor and directory pages cited in section 2):

- Runs an affiliate program on Awin, Rakuten, CJ or impact.com, paying single-digit to low-teens commission to publishers and creators; sees clicks and conversions per publisher, never per recipient.
- Runs a referral program (Mention Me, Yotpo, ReferralCandy, Social Snowball) that captures incentivised sharing after purchase; sees referrer and referee per brand.
- Runs an ambassador or creator program (Duel, Brandbassador, SocialLadder, Superfiliate) with tasks, codes, storefronts and UGC; sees program members and their attributed sales.
- Sponsors run clubs, studios and events directly with product seeding and perks; measures nothing beyond code redemptions and content.
- Buys Strava challenges or club presence; measures participation.
- Learns about organic word of mouth through "how did you hear about us" surveys and anecdotes.

Why this is often fine:

- The incentivised part of word of mouth is already captured. Referral programs exist precisely to convert private recommendations into tracked ones for that brand.
- The unmeasured remainder is treated as brand halo. Marketing teams are used to it and budget for it as "community" or "field".
- Software cost is zero for the organic layer, and the team already exists.
- The tools above integrate with Shopify and Klaviyo and are known quantities to the buyer.

What "doing nothing" leaves invisible, which is the argument Sway must make with evidence rather than assertion: which specific non-customers and non-creators are moving product through which communities, whether their recommendations reach people the brand cannot otherwise reach, and whether recognising them changes behaviour. No public source in this research quantifies that gap for wellness. It is a hypothesis.

## 6. Five alternatives the founders should personally try before Friday 9 October

Each script is 15 minutes, needs only a phone, and produces a one-page note in the same format so the notes can sit side by side on Saturday morning. Record: date, device, what you tried, time taken per step, the exact screen where you hesitated, one screenshot per step, and a one-line answer to "what would Sway have to do better here?"

Suggested split: Tory takes 1, 4 and 5 (recipient and commercial lens); Blake takes 2 and 3 (product and build lens). Swap phones for the scan tests.

### Test 1: ShopMy shopper app and Circles (the trust-signal incumbent)

Setup: install the ShopMy shopper app (App Store or Google Play), or use shopmy.us on mobile. Sign up as a shopper, not a creator.

1. (3 min) Search for three wellness items you actually own (running shoes, electrolyte, a supplement). Note whether the results are creator-led or product-led, and whether wellness has depth or is thin next to fashion and beauty.
2. (4 min) Build a Circle. Try to add (a) a well-known creator, (b) a friend who is not a creator. Record whether (b) is possible at all. The PDF says Circles can include "friends"; confirm what "friend" means in the product.
3. (3 min) Open a creator's profile. Find the Trusted By count. Tap it. Record what it is made of and whether it is explained to a shopper.
4. (3 min) Attempt to share one product to a friend by WhatsApp. Record what the friend receives (link format, who is credited, whether a reason or note travels with it).
5. (2 min) Look for any in-person or QR share option. Record "none found" if none.

Look for: the moment where a non-creator's trust is invisible; whether a recipient can see "why". These are the two gaps Sway claims.

Fallback if the app is region-locked: use the web version; if that fails, read https://shopmy.us/blog/post/introducing-circles-discovery-reimagined and the creator guide FAQ and note that the test was desk-based.

### Test 2: LTK consumer public profile and Lists (the creator-first incumbent)

### Test 3: Build a five-product storefront in Beacons (or Linktree Shops if a US account is available)

2. (3 min) Publish and open the page on the other founder's phone. Time from tap to product page.

4. (2 min) Try to generate a QR code for the page inside the product. Record whether it exists.
5. (2 min) Note the plan needed to keep 100% of commission and its price.

Look for: how much of Sway's "Locker plus Sway Link" is already a free commodity, and where the commodity stops (no recipient identity, no reason, no IRL).

Fallback: if sign-up needs a social login you do not want to connect, use https://beacons.ai/i/pricing and the April 2025 marketplace release and mark the test desk-based.

### Test 4: Be the recipient of a Mention Me Name Share and an impact.com or Extole QR referral

2. (5 min) QR referral: scan the QR with the camera. Record the landing page, whether the referrer is named, whether a reason is shown, and how many taps to the product.
3. (3 min) Compare both against Sway's recipient journey in 02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md (recognisable product, who recommends, short reason, benefit, one dominant action). Score each of the five elements present or absent.
4. (2 min) Write one line: what would a run-club member need on the landing page that neither of these shows?

Fallback: if no live Mention Me checkout can be found in time, read https://help.mention-me.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360005869717-How-referees-use-name-sharing (blocked in this environment; may work on a phone) and mark desk-based.

### Test 5: Make a personal Sway Pass with no code, and scan it

Setup: iPhone with the current iOS. If iOS 27 has shipped with "Create a Pass" in Wallet, use it. If not, use a free pass generator (QR to Wallet at https://qrtowallet.com/ or Walletsio from the App Store) or simply a QR code of a personal link printed on paper.

1. (4 min) Create a QR that resolves to a simple personal page (the Beacons page from Test 3 is fine). Put it in Wallet via Create a Pass or a generator. Record the steps and whether the pass looks like something you would show a stranger.
2. (3 min) Other founder scans it from the lock screen at arm's length, indoors and outdoors. Time from "can I see that?" to product page. Repeat three times.
3. (3 min) Now scan it and try to select a specific product and see a reason. Record where the flow breaks (this is the part Sway must build).
4. (3 min) Ask the other founder, as a run-club member: "Would you show this to someone after a run?" Record the verbatim answer and the first objection.
5. (2 min) Note whether Apple's Create a Pass, if shipped, makes the "Add to Wallet" part of Sway a commodity. Read https://developer.apple.com/wallet/loyalty-passes/ for what a developer-issued pass can additionally do (dynamic updates, location-triggered notifications).

Look for: how much of the Pass experience is free platform behaviour versus Sway's own work; the real social friction of showing a QR in person.

### Optional test 6, 15 minutes of observation instead of a product

In one wellness WhatsApp group each founder is already in, ask a genuine question ("what do people use for post-run recovery?") and watch for 48 hours. Record: number of replies, format of each (text only, brand link, Amazon link, screenshot, code), and whether anyone asks "can you send me the link?". This is the "doing nothing" baseline.
