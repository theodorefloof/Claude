# The founders' brief (verbatim requirements, restated)

Today is Wednesday 16 September 2026. Workshop year is 2026.
Arrive Barcelona evening Fri 9 Oct 2026. Workshop Sat 10 and Sun 11 Oct 2026. Fly home Sun 11 Oct (time unknown).
Barcelona is CEST (UTC+2) in October; UK is BST (UTC+1).

Participants (no external facilitator):
- Blake ("Me") - product lead: product definition, UX, product priorities, prototype development (AI-assisted vibe coding). VP of Product at Duel (duel.tech) in his day job.
- Tory - sales lead and originator of the idea: commercial thinking, customer/buyer understanding, positioning, sales strategy, original vision.
Do not assume either has technical implementation skills beyond what the context establishes.

Constraints:
- Second day must finish in time for packing, travel to airport, appropriate airport buffer.
- Protect dinner and recovery time on evenings in Barcelona.
- Arrival evening light and optional. Essential preparation completed beforehand.
- Realistic breaks, lunch, transitions, contingency.
- Do not silently assume two full workshop days. If logistics missing, provide clearly labelled provisional schedule and identify assumptions.
- Establish: year; when prep can begin; realistic daily availability for prep; return flight time and airport transfer constraints.

Five questions to answer with a shared, specific, defensible answer:
1. What is Sway? (category, target customer, founding insight, positioning, reason to exist)
2. What does it do? (core UX, outcome, how it works, what belongs in first product)
3. How do we sell it? (user, buyer, budget holder, purchase trigger, value demo, pricing/packaging hypotheses, first customers)
4. How do we raise capital? (investment case, scale, why now, why us, evidence investors need, milestones capital funds)
5. How do we make it dramatically better than a platform like Duel? (translate "a million times better" into a meaningful advantage for a specific customer: outcomes, experience, speed, economics, capabilities; direct competition AND different ways of solving the underlying problem)

Also: a focused working prototype built via AI-assisted vibe coding during the workshop, demonstrating Sway's central promise. It makes the concept tangible; it does not validate demand.

Required final outputs:
1. One-page company thesis
2. Product definition (core experience, primary journey, outcome, explicit exclusions)
3. Differentiation thesis (vs Duel, other alternatives, doing nothing; demonstrable vs hypothetical)
4. Initial sales strategy (ICP, buyer vs user, trigger, positioning, initial offer, pricing hypotheses, acquisition, objections, first-customer plan)
5. Fundraising narrative and slide-by-slide deck draft (headlines, key points, supporting evidence, evidence gaps; argument before polish; DO NOT invent amount or valuation)
6. Product priorities (long-term vision vs initial MVP vs workshop prototype)
7. Working prototype (one end-to-end core journey, short demo script, known limitations)
8. Execution plan (decisions, owners, unresolved questions, next 7 days of discovery/product/sales/fundraising prep)

Required response structure:
1. Success criteria and programme logic (minimum successful outcome, stretch goals, maturity of each deliverable)
2. Master calendar and deadline register (table: Deliverable | Owner | Reviewer | Inputs required | Due date and time | Completion criterion | Fallback)
3. Daily preparation agendas per founder (instructions + output formats)
4. Detailed agendas for 10 and 11 Oct: scannable table (Time | Activity | My role | Tory's role | Output or decision due) then a runbook per session (purpose/question, inputs, step-by-step with internal timeboxes, exact prompts, leadership/recording/artifact ownership, tangible output, completion criteria, fallback)
5. Prototype delivery plan (journey selection, scope lock time, must work / may be mocked / deferred, build brief for AI coding tool, Blake's implementation responsibilities and Tory's parallel work, checkpoints, testing/fix/final demo time, scope reductions on missed checkpoints)
6. Copy-ready templates tied to sessions, reusing information
7. Final review design and dated seven-day follow-through plan (owner, time estimate, output, completion criterion)

Preparation requirements:
- Separate essential individual, essential shared, optional.
- 60-90 min independent strategic thinking per founder; separately estimate customer conversations, competitive research, material gathering, technical setup.
- For every prep task: exact instructions, owner, duration, output format, deadline, workshop use, fallback if incomplete.
- Shared workspace structure and technical readiness check.
- Blake prep: product hypothesis & core journey; candidate first experiences & scope boundaries; existing mockups/notes/tools; prototype environment readiness & constraints; major product assumptions.
- Tory prep: founding insight; customer & buyer hypotheses; existing customer conversations & evidence of demand; alternatives, objections, purchase triggers; positioning, offer, pricing hypotheses, routes to first customers.
- Both independently: short description of Sway; the customer problem that matters most; why a customer would choose it; ambitions, constraints, biggest doubts; what the other founder may see differently; what evidence would change their mind.

Competitive thinking: confirm which Duel is intended; use public primary sources, cite; separate what Duel demonstrably offers / what customers value or struggle with (with evidence) / where Sway could differ / why switch, use alongside, or adopt first / what is hard to copy. Do not assume Duel is weak or that more features = better.

Workshop design principles: organise around consequential decisions; sequence so each session feeds the next; short independent exercises before discussion; alternate leadership; specify who leads/records/owns; decision rule for disagreements; record uncertain decisions with review trigger; distinguish decisions/drafts/demonstrations/validated findings; protect implementation time with scope lock; parallel product+commercial work only where dependencies allow, with reconciliation points; hard final review deadline before travel cutoff; reduce scope deliberately when short (never remove breaks, dinner, or risk departure); protect the five answers and a demonstrable core experience; polish and peripheral features are stretch.

Audit before presenting: timings add up; two people can do it; dependencies satisfied; implementation time sufficient; every deliverable has owner+deadline; day two finishes safely before travel cutoff; recovery plan for missed checkpoints.

Style: plain language, concrete instructions, explicit deadlines, no padding, no generic startup advice. Australian English. No em dashes. Do not invent customer evidence, traction, market data, or validation. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, unanswered questions.
