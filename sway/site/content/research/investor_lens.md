# Investor lens: what a credible pre-seed or seed narrative for Sway must contain

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Sway founders' workshop in Barcelona (Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026).
Written from the seat of a seed-stage investor and former founder. It defines what the raise is actually selling, gives a slide-by-slide skeleton with evidence classes, sets out milestone logic without amounts, lists the diligence items on the founders, and records which external statistics could and could not be sourced.

No amount, valuation, traction figure, price or market size is proposed anywhere in this file. Where the Sway PDFs contain illustrative numbers they are quoted only to say what not to put in front of an investor.

## 1. What this raise is really selling

### 1.1 The honest position on 16 September 2026

There is no traction. There are no users, no revenue, no signed brands, no waitlist figure in the documents, and no data from a live product. What exists is:

- A thesis and a canonical product spine: MY 5 → LOCKER → SWAY PASS/LINK → RECOMMEND → EARN → TRUST → SWAY FOUNDING MEMBER COLLECTIVE → TRUST MAP → BRAND RECOGNITION → BRAND COLLECTIVE → ACTIVATION → SCALE. [FOUNDER ASSERTION]
- A narrow, approved MVP boundary: account → My 5 → Locker → public profile or recommendation → Sway Link → QR or Sway Pass → basic interaction tracking. [FOUNDER ASSERTION]
- A clickable prototype at a chatgpt.site subdomain, described by the founders themselves as "an evolving discussion artifact, not a production product". [FOUNDER ASSERTION, 06_SOURCE_INDEX.md]
- A working prototype to be built at the workshop, which the brief states "makes the concept tangible; it does not validate demand". [FOUNDER ASSERTION, 00_BRIEF.md]
- Four historical PDFs with a commercial architecture whose numbers the founders already label as "planning assumptions to validate, not contracted prices, forecasts, or settled policy". [FOUNDER ASSERTION, 04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md]
- Two founders: Tory (sales lead, originator of the idea) and Blake (product lead, currently VP of Product at Duel, a funded incumbent in the category). Neither is established as full time on Sway in the documents. [FOUNDER ASSERTION, 00_BRIEF.md]

That is a pre-seed position. Call it that. A "seed" label invites a comparison with companies that have a live cohort and a retention curve, and Sway will lose that comparison in the first meeting. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

### 1.2 What an investor is actually buying

With no traction, the cheque buys three things: a thesis with a sharp wedge, a team that can execute it cheaply, and a plan that converts a small amount of money into evidence fast. The deck exists to make those three legible. It does not exist to prove the business works; nothing available today can do that.

The thesis, in one sentence the founders already own: Sway turns trusted product recommendations into measurable commerce. [FOUNDER ASSERTION, 01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md]

The wedge: measurable word of mouth for wellness brands, built through dense wellness and healthy-living communities in one city, with the consumer product useful before any brand pays. [FOUNDER ASSERTION]

The shape: a consumer network first, monetised later through brands (intelligence, activation, scale). This shape is the hardest to fund at pre-seed because it asks a consumer investor to underwrite a B2B exit and a B2B investor to underwrite consumer behaviour. The narrative must not hedge between the two. Lead with the consumer loop and treat the brand revenue as the reason the loop is worth funding, not as a near-term line item. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

### 1.3 The three things investors will need to believe

**Belief 1. Ordinary people will build and use a recommendation identity without being paid to.**
The specific behaviour: complete My 5, keep adding to the Locker, and reach for the Sway Link or Sway Pass when someone asks "what do you recommend". The founders' own first-proof test is exactly this (01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md, "The first proof is not that Sway can offer every form of advocacy. It is that people will: build a useful recommendation identity; use it naturally when someone asks; generate trackable scans, clicks, saves and purchases; return"). [FOUNDER ASSERTION]
Evidence today: none. The strongest indirect external support is that people already recommend constantly and privately (Nielsen 2021: "88% of consumers said that they trusted recommendations from people they know, above all other forms", 40,000 people, 56 countries, https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2021/beyond-martech-building-trust-with-consumers-and-engaging-where-sentiment-is-high/ [SNIPPET]; a HubSpot survey re-cited by a third party as 61 percent preferring to share recommendations privately, https://paxcom.ai/blog/dark-social-for-ecommerce-brands/ [THIRD-PARTY, dated]). That proves the behaviour exists, not that people will route it through a product.
What closes it: a first cohort using the MVP, with the metrics in 04_COMMERCIAL_AND_LAUNCH.md ("Metrics for the first proof") reported honestly.

**Belief 2. The recommendation event can be captured honestly, and what it reveals is worth money to brands that the current stack cannot see.**
Evidence today, all indirect: brands measure word of mouth badly (post-purchase surveys show "word-of-mouth first discovery is 2-3x higher than their last-click attribution model suggests", with one example of "2% in Platform Data versus 23% in Survey Data", https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide and https://www.attnagency.com/blog/post-purchase-survey-attribution [SNIPPET]); measuring ROI is the top-ranked challenge in creator marketing ("52% of marketers ranking determining ROI of influencer campaigns as their top challenge", https://www.aspire.io/blog/influencer-marketing-challenges-and-solutions [SNIPPET]); and one public Duel review names "the difficulty in proving emotional advocacy's financial worth to the business" (https://www.g2.com/products/duel/reviews, September 2024 [SNIPPET]). No brand was found publicly asking for in-person recommendation attribution (research/duel_customer_evidence.md, Gap 4). [NEEDS SOURCE for direct brand demand]
What closes it: verbatim answers from three to five wellness brand marketers to the questions in research/duel_customer_evidence.md section 6a, especially questions 4 and 5, and one brand agreeing to look at pilot data.

**Belief 3. These two founders can get from zero to a dense community pilot on a small cheque, and at least one of them will be full time.**
Evidence today: the workshop prototype (proves the product lead can ship a demo with AI-assisted tooling), and the founders' complementary roles. Against it: neither founder is shown as full time, and one is a senior employee of a funded incumbent. Section 4 treats this in full.
What closes it: a signed founder agreement (05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md lists this as a required output), a stated date by which one founder is full time, and evidence of community recruitment done personally (leaders contacted, sessions attended, interviews held).

### 1.4 The belief not to try to prove yet

Investors will also want to believe the affiliate-first economics work. Do not spend deck space proving it. The public rails in the launch category pay little: HOKA reported at 5 percent full price and 3 percent sale (https://www.hoka.com/en/us/hoka-affiliate-program.html [SNIPPET, conflicting third-party claims of up to 14 percent]); Amazon Associates health and personal care reported at 1 percent (https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/ [THIRD-PARTY]); lululemon about 5 to 10 percent (https://www.creator-hero.com/blog/lululemon-affiliate-program-in-depth-review-pros-and-cons [THIRD-PARTY]); supplement programmes 8 to 12 percent (https://getlasso.co/niche/supplements/ [THIRD-PARTY]). On those rates the advocate's share of a typical purchase is small. Say so. Position affiliate as the bridge that lets the network exist before brands pay, exactly as the strategy already does ("Use available affiliate infrastructure where appropriate, while clearly labelling eligibility and pending/confirmed/reversed outcomes", 01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md), and let the brand layers carry the economics. An investor who hears "we make money from affiliate on day one" will run the maths and stop listening. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

### 1.5 What seed investors in 2025 to 2026 look for in this shape [PRACTITIONER VIEW, no citation; searches could not be run]

For a consumer network with later B2B monetisation, the pre-seed and seed conversation turns on engagement and retention signals, not revenue. Specifically, for Sway:

- A core action defined and counted: "an attributable recommendation created" (link shared or Pass scanned) per active member per week. Not "app opens".
- Retention of the first cohort at 7 and 30 days, on the core action, not on logins. A small cohort with a flat curve after week two beats a large cohort that decays to zero.
- Recipient-to-member conversion: what share of people who scan or click become members. This is the organic growth engine the strategy depends on and the only cheap proxy for a viral coefficient.
- Density in one node: what share of a run club or studio's regulars are members, and whether recommendations cross between nodes.
- Qualitative pull: people asking to join without being asked, and community leaders inviting Sway in. Verbatim quotes with dates.
- On the brand side, at pre-seed nobody expects paying brands. They do expect brand pull: a handful of marketers saying, on the record, "if you can show me X I would pay for it", and one willing to look at pilot data.
- A cold-start plan that does not require the other side: the founders already have this (consumer useful first, Sway-funded Founding Member rewards, affiliate rails for eligible products). Investors will test whether the Founding Member rewards are affordable and whether the product is useful with zero rewards.

The founders should try to source published benchmarks for these signals themselves (for example consumer retention benchmarks published by venture firms or newsletters) and cite them; none could be retrieved here. [NEEDS SOURCE]

## 2. Slide-by-slide skeleton (14 slides)

Argument before polish. Each slide gives: title, draft headline, key points, evidence available today with its label, the evidence gap and how the next four to eight weeks could close it (four weeks from today is Wednesday 14 October 2026, eight weeks is Wednesday 11 November 2026; anything needed for the workshop is due by Thursday 8 October), and what not to claim.

### Slide 1. Title

- Headline (draft): "Sway turns trusted product recommendations into measurable commerce."
- Key points: one line, founders' names and roles, date, "pre-seed discussion draft". No tagline about AI, no "trust infrastructure for real-world commerce" (that phrase promises infrastructure that does not exist yet).
- Evidence today: the one-thing sentence. [FOUNDER ASSERTION]
- Gap and closure: none needed.
- Do not claim: a category name Sway invented, a company stage beyond pre-seed, or any figure.

### Slide 2. The problem, seen from both sides

- Headline (draft): "People recommend products constantly. Almost all of it vanishes, for the person and for the brand."
- Key points: (1) Recommendation is the most trusted input to a purchase. (2) It mostly happens privately or in person, where no tool sees it. (3) Brands measure it with surveys because their pixels cannot; the gap between survey and pixel is large. (4) The person who recommended gets nothing but social credit. (5) The people who move products in communities are often not creators and are invisible to creator tools.
- Evidence today: Nielsen 2021, 88 percent trust recommendations from people they know (https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2021/beyond-martech-building-trust-with-consumers-and-engaging-where-sentiment-is-high/ [SNIPPET]). McKinsey, word of mouth "the primary factor behind 20 to 50 percent of all purchasing decisions" (2010 article, https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/a-new-way-to-measure-word-of-mouth-marketing [SNIPPET, dated]). Fairing survey-versus-pixel gap, 2 percent versus 23 percent in one example (https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide [SNIPPET]). Aspire, 52 percent rank ROI determination as the top challenge (https://www.aspire.io/blog/influencer-marketing-challenges-and-solutions [SNIPPET]). APMA Voice of the Nation 2025, 59 percent of brands report difficulty measuring how affiliate affects other channels (https://www.affiversemedia.com/apma-voice-of-the-nation-2025-five-insights-reshaping-affiliate-strategy/ [SNIPPET]). Referral gap, "83% of satisfied customers are willing to refer products and services, yet only 29% actually do" (Texas Tech survey, widely re-cited, methodology not retrieved, https://growsurf.com/statistics/referral-marketing-statistics/ [THIRD-PARTY]).
- Gap and closure: every figure above is snippet-grade and none is wellness-specific. Before 8 October, Tory opens each primary page in a normal browser, confirms the wording and year, and records it. Add two or three verbatim quotes from the brand-marketer conversations (research/duel_customer_evidence.md section 6a) and two from community members (section 6b).
- Do not claim: a wellness word-of-mouth statistic (none found), or that "brands are asking for IRL attribution" (no public evidence).

### Slide 3. Why now

- Headline (draft): "Creator commerce built the rails. Trust is now the scarce signal. Nobody has claimed the person-to-person layer."
- Key points: (1) Affiliate and creator-commerce infrastructure is mature and open to more people than before: ShopMy reported 175,000+ creators and 1,000+ brand partners at July 2025 (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/shopmy-launches-shopping-platform-a-personalized-destination-built-by-the-creators-you-trust-302516804.html [SNIPPET]); impact.com added customer referrals to its partnership platform on 30 July 2024 and made creator Storefronts generally available on 11 June 2026 (https://impact.com/press-releases/impact-com-unveils-advocate-referral-solution/ and https://impact.com/press-releases/impact-unveils-ai-creator-commerce-ipx/ [SNIPPET]). (2) Consumer-side "trust" products launched in the last fourteen months: ShopMy Circles and Trusted By (30 July 2025, https://shopmy.us/blog/post/introducing-circles-discovery-reimagined [SNIPPET]) and LTK public consumer profiles and Lists (30 July 2025, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250730281472/en/LTK-Announces-New-LTK-App-Features-Expanding-Its-Role-as-a-Social-Platform-for-Creator-Led-Communities [SNIPPET]). The market is moving toward trust as a signal, which validates the direction and raises the bar. (3) Investors are funding the category: Duel announced a USD 16 million Series A on 23 September 2025 co-led by Molten Ventures and Bright Pixel (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250923705563/en/Brand-Advocacy-Platform-Duel-Raises-$16M-in-Series-A [SNIPPET, press release via syndication]). (4) The phone has become a pass wallet: Apple's reported iOS 27 "Create a Pass" lets a user turn any QR code into a Wallet pass without a developer account (https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/09/how-apple-wallets-create-a-pass-works-in-ios-27 [THIRD-PARTY, shipping status as of today UNVERIFIED]). (5) AI-generated content is making manufactured recommendation cheap, so credible human signal becomes scarcer. [FOUNDER ASSERTION in 02_EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY; volume statistic NEEDS SOURCE]
- Evidence today: items 1 to 4 have URLs at snippet grade. Item 5 has no sourced figure.
- Gap and closure: find and verify one primary statistic on AI-generated content volume or on consumer trust in AI-generated versus human recommendations; if none is verifiable by 8 October, drop item 5 from the slide and keep it as a spoken point. Verify the ShopMy and LTK dates on the primary pages.
- Do not claim: a creator-economy or affiliate-industry market size (none sourced), that AI content "will be 90 percent of the internet" (widely repeated, unverified here), or that incumbents "cannot" build the person-to-person layer.

### Slide 4. The insight and the wedge

- Headline (draft): "Start one layer under creator commerce: the recommendation between two real people, online and in person, owned by the person who made it."
- Key points: (1) Anyone can have influence; follower count is not the qualifier. (2) One identity, one Locker across brands, one Pass for in-person moments and one Link for digital. (3) The event chain: advocate → product → recommendation → recipient → action → purchase where visible. (4) Wellness communities are the acquisition nodes because recommendation already happens there weekly; wellness is the territory, not the boundary. (5) Consumer value first; brands second.
- Evidence today: all [FOUNDER ASSERTION]. External support for the gap: in 49 searches no product was found that does consumer-owned, cross-brand, person-to-recipient attribution end to end; the closest are brand-scoped referral tools (Mention Me Name Share, impact.com QR referral, Extole receipt QR, Talkable wallet pass), each one pass per brand (research/alternatives_landscape.md section 4, URLs there [SNIPPET]). Absence of evidence in searches is not proof of absence.
- Gap and closure: the founders re-run the section 4 searches themselves before the workshop and personally try ShopMy's shopper app and LTK Lists (research/alternatives_landscape.md section 6, tests 1 and 2) so the "nobody does this" line is first-hand and dated.
- Do not claim: "no one else can do this", "first mover", or that ShopMy and LTK have no consumer trust features (both shipped them in July 2025).

### Slide 5. The product

- Headline (draft): "Five things worth the money becomes a Locker, a Link and a Pass. One scan turns a conversation into an attributable recommendation."
- Key points: the canonical spine up to the first proof (MY 5 → LOCKER → SWAY PASS/LINK → RECOMMEND → EARN → TRUST); the two journeys (advocate adds and recommends; recipient lands on a fast page with the product, who recommends it, the reason, one dominant action, and an optional save or join); the explicit do-not-build list (Brand Command Center, AI segmentation, Content Studio, enterprise integrations, wallet sophistication). Show three or four screens, or the live prototype.
- Evidence today: the workshop prototype and the clickable prototype. Both are demonstrations. The brief is explicit that the prototype does not validate demand. [FOUNDER ASSERTION]
- Gap and closure: a short recorded demo on two real phones (the prototype plan in research/prototype_feasibility.md ends with acceptance tests on two phones). Between 14 October and 11 November, put the prototype in front of ten community members and record what they do unprompted.
- Do not claim: Apple or Google Wallet passes exist (they will be mocked; a real Apple pass needs the paid developer programme and certificate signing, https://developer.apple.com/programs/ [FETCHED]), that affiliate tracking is live, that purchases are attributed, or that the map shows real geography.

### Slide 6. Attribution honesty: what we capture and what we do not

- Headline (draft): "We record observable events and say what we do not know. Pending, confirmed and reversed are three different words."
- Key points: (1) Events captured: brand, product, channel, community context, timestamp, approximate city when consented, repeat behaviour. (2) A scan is not a purchase; a click is not a purchase; a purchase is attributed only where an affiliate or brand programme reports it, and it can be reversed within the network's locking period. (3) Trust is evidence that people acted because of a recommendation; Sway Points reward participation; they are not the same thing. (4) The recipient page never promises a discount unless terms actually provide one.
- Evidence today: the language discipline in 00_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md and 02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md ("Do not infer a purchase from a click or a trusted relationship from raw activity alone"). [FOUNDER ASSERTION] External context: LTK reported as "30-day, last-click attribution" with "returns claw money back" (https://brandid.app/reviews/ltk/ [THIRD-PARTY]); ShopMy pays creators weekly on a stated 82/18 split (https://guide.shopmy.us/289138ecba514337965ea05d1896d1e7 [SNIPPET]).
- Gap and closure: write the attribution event definitions at the workshop (Day 2, item 3 in 05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md) and put the table on this slide. Confirm the locking period and reversal rules of the one affiliate network chosen for the pilot.
- Do not claim: "we attribute in-person purchases" as a present-tense capability, a match rate, or that Trust is a validated metric.

### Slide 7. Go-to-market: one city, dense nodes, consumers first

- Headline (draft): "Recruit Founding Members where recommendation already happens weekly. Build density in one city before anything else."
- Key points: (1) Node types: run clubs, Pilates and yoga, boutique fitness and strength, cycling and training groups, recovery spaces, healthy-living events (03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN). (2) The founders recruit personally through community leaders. (3) The Sway Founding Member Collective bridges the cold start with Sway-funded recognition and rewards that feel like access, not couponing. (4) Brands are approached only when the data is surprising: "Your community already exists. We found it." (5) Expansion is within the city, not national.
- Evidence today: the plan. [FOUNDER ASSERTION] The city, the nodes and the Founding Member benefit are open questions the founders must settle (05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md, "Which city and which 3 to 5 community nodes launch first?").
- Gap and closure: by 8 October, a named launch city, a map of 40 to 50 communities ranked to a top ten (the PDF's own week-one plan), five to ten leaders contacted, at least three gatherings attended, and ten five-minute intercept conversations recorded (research/duel_customer_evidence.md Gap 5). By 11 November, a written list of the first 25 to 50 Founding Members with consent to be contacted.
- Do not claim: a member count, a waitlist figure, a partnership with any club or studio, or a CAC.

### Slide 8. Business model: layers, not a price list

- Headline (draft): "Consumers never pay. Commerce funds the network early. Brands pay for intelligence, activation and scale once the community is real."
- Key points: (1) Layer 1 commerce: eligible affiliate commission shared with the advocate on a simple, advocate-favouring rule. (2) Layer 2 intelligence: a brand pays to see its organic advocacy. (3) Layer 3 activation: a brand pays to run a Collective. (4) Layer 4 scale: automation, rights, segmentation, enterprise operations. (5) The advocate's share does not shrink as Sway monetises brands.
- Evidence today: the architecture. [FOUNDER ASSERTION] Public benchmarks that make the advocate share credible: ShopMy 82/18 (https://guide.shopmy.us/289138ecba514337965ea05d1896d1e7 [SNIPPET]); LTK's guidance to brands that 13 to 15 percent creator commission is "reasonable" and 16 to 20 percent "high and competitive", recommending 14 percent (https://onbrand.shopltk.com/how-to-choose-the-right-commission-rate-for-influencers [SNIPPET]). Real wellness rates are far lower (section 1.4).
- Gap and closure: at the workshop, decide whether the 80/20 rule is a principle to state or a number to withhold. In the brand conversations, ask what they pay today and for what (research/duel_customer_evidence.md Gap 6). Rebase every worked example on real rates for the products the first cohort actually puts in their Lockers.
- Do not claim: the USD 30K, 75K and 150K package prices [PLANNING ASSUMPTION], the 16 to 20 percent "Collective target" as achievable in wellness (no wellness brand found paying it to non-creators, research/alternatives_landscape.md section 5), the 20 percent content fee or 10 percent reward fee [PLANNING ASSUMPTION], the USD 540K per brand scenario [PLANNING ASSUMPTION], or any revenue forecast.

### Slide 10. Why it compounds

- Headline (draft): "Every credible recommendation makes the map more useful. The mechanism is the QR and the link. The asset is the history."
- Key points: (1) The Trust Map: people, products, recommendations, recipients, communities, actions, purchases where visible, geography, time and repeat behaviour. (2) It is cross-brand and person-owned, which is what brand-scoped tools structurally cannot accumulate. (3) It becomes valuable through density, so the first city is the whole test. (4) Category-specific trust: the same person is trusted for running shoes and not for skincare.
- Evidence today: hypothesis. [FOUNDER ASSERTION] The founders' own document says the moat "cannot remain positioning language" and must become product architecture (01_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.txt).
- Gap and closure: nothing in eight weeks proves a data moat. ### 4.4 Disclosure item three: the regulatory and privacy posture, stated before it is asked

The following is written at the level of principle from prior knowledge. Every primary page was blocked in this environment and no wording is quoted. The founders' lawyer should read the primary sources listed.

- Affiliate recommendations as advertising, United States: the Federal Trade Commission's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised in 2023) treat a commission or other benefit to the person recommending as a material connection that must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously; the FTC's business guidance on the Guides is at https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking [UNVERIFIED, primary page blocked; verify wording and date]. The consequence for Sway: the recipient page and any shared link must show, before the recipient acts, that the recommender may earn if they buy. Sway's own product model already requires that the recipient page state "the recipient benefit, if one genuinely exists" and never promise a discount that terms do not provide; disclosure of the recommender's benefit must be added to that page from the first version. [FOUNDER ASSERTION plus PRACTITIONER VIEW]
- Affiliate recommendations as advertising, United Kingdom: the Advertising Standards Authority and the Committee of Advertising Practice treat affiliate content as a marketing communication that must be obviously identifiable as such under the CAP Code (the ASA's advice page is at https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html) [UNVERIFIED, primary page blocked; verify rule numbers and wording]. The practical form in the UK is an upfront label such as "Ad" on affiliate content. The same design consequence applies.
- Personal data and location, United Kingdom and European Union: the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR both set out the data protection principles in Article 5(1); the two most relevant to a Trust Map are purpose limitation (Article 5(1)(b)) and data minimisation (Article 5(1)(c)), with lawfulness, fairness and transparency (Article 5(1)(a)) governing what members and recipients are told (UK text at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/5, EU text at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679, ICO guide at https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/) [UNVERIFIED, primary pages blocked; cite the article numbers only after checking]. The product model's choice of "approximate city-level influence" without exposing exact location, and the plan to mock rather than collect precise geolocation in the prototype (research/prototype_feasibility.md section 6), are the right starting posture. The recipient is also a data subject: a scan creates a record about a person who has not joined Sway, and the narrative should say what is stored about them and for how long.
- Payouts and money movement: paying advocates creates tax, identity and payment obligations that vary by country; affiliate networks commonly modify or reverse actions during a locking period (04_REVENUE_MODEL.txt notes this) and Sway's pending, confirmed and reversed states must match the network's rules. [FOUNDER ASSERTION plus PRACTITIONER VIEW]

Investors do not expect these solved at pre-seed. They expect the founders to know they exist and to have designed the recipient page with disclosure in it from day one. [PRACTITIONER VIEW]

## 5. Investor objections, the best honest answer today, and what would strengthen it

Eighteen objections. Each answer uses only what is true on 16 September 2026.

1. **"ShopMy or LTK adds this in a quarter."**
   Honest answer today: ShopMy shipped Circles and Trusted By in July 2025 and LTK shipped consumer Lists the same month; both measure trust as digital acts toward creators, gate visibility to established creators (ShopMy search requires Trendsetter or Icon status or Trusted By above 100), and neither has a person-owned, cross-brand, in-person record or lets an ordinary consumer earn and be recognised by a brand as far as found (https://guide.shopmy.us/circles-wishlist-latest-finds-and-more/5jLY2jskGAENwEWJEf147B/faqs/5kdVRoGw9nmmppW3uMKyRZ ; https://company.shopltk.com/en-gb/faqs [SNIPPET]). Their business is creator monetisation; opening to everyone dilutes the creator proposition they sell to brands. That is a strategic reason, not a technical barrier, and it could change.
   Strengthened by: first-hand tests of both products, dated; evidence that Sway's members are mostly people those platforms would reject (no public following).

2. **"Duel just raised USD 16 million and already sells advocacy to beauty and wellness brands."**
   Honest answer today: from public sources, Duel sells a brand-owned programme: the brand signs first, recruits advocates into its programme, and the advocate's history lives inside that programme (https://www.duel.tech/how-it-works/advocate-portal ; https://www.duel.tech/duel-brand-advocacy-solution [SNIPPET]). Sway starts with the person, before any brand pays, across brands, and in person. Different job, different first customer, and the two could coexist. Duel's funding is evidence that brands pay for advocacy, which helps Sway's later layers. The founders will not use anything but public information about Duel, and they say so.
   Strengthened by: brand marketers saying what a brand-owned programme cannot show them (research/duel_customer_evidence.md question 5); the disclosure plan in section 4.2 handled cleanly.

3. **"Two-sided cold start. Consumers will not use it before brands, and brands will not pay before consumers."**
   Honest answer today: the design assumes exactly that and requires the consumer product to be valuable alone: My 5, Locker, Link and Pass are useful with zero brands; affiliate rails cover eligible products; Sway funds Founding Member recognition (01_CURRENT_STRATEGY.md, 03_PHASED_LAUNCH_PLAN.txt). Brands are approached only with data. Nothing yet proves the consumer side.
   Strengthened by: M1 proof points, especially retention on the core action and recipient-to-member conversion.

4. **"Affiliate rates in wellness are 1 to 12 percent. The advocate earns cents."**
   Honest answer today: correct on the public rates (section 1.4). Affiliate is the bridge that lets recommendations be eligible for anything at all; it is not the advocate's reason to use Sway. The revenue model's 10 percent "standard rate" is above wellness reality and will be rebased.
   Strengthened by: community members saying what they would want in return (research/duel_customer_evidence.md 6b, question 5); pilot data on whether members without any earnings still return.

5. **"An in-person scan does not prove a purchase. Brands will call your numbers soft."**
   Honest answer today: agreed, and the product never infers a purchase from a scan or a click (02_PRODUCT_MODEL.md). Sway reports observable events with their status. The claim to a brand is not "we attribute in-person sales"; it is "we can show you who is recommending you, to whom, where, and what observable action followed, which no current tool shows". The survey-versus-pixel gap (2 percent versus 23 percent, https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide [SNIPPET]) is why brands might value even partial visibility.
   Strengthened by: the attribution event table from the workshop; brand marketers' answers to "the single number your CFO believes".

6. **"Nobody shows a friend a QR code. It feels salesy."**
   Honest answer today: the risk is real; category commentary warns that commissions can "flatten the relationship into a payout system" (https://brandchamp.io/blog/ambassador-marketing-beyond-affiliate-rewards/ [SNIPPET]) and the referral gap (83 percent willing, 29 percent do, [THIRD-PARTY]) shows friction in acting on goodwill. Untested.
   Strengthened by: ten intercept conversations asking question 7 ("Would you show a QR or link on your phone to a friend who asked? What would stop you?"); observed behaviour with the prototype.
