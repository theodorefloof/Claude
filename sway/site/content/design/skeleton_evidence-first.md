# Sway programme skeleton: evidence-first design

Prepared Wednesday 16 September 2026 for the Barcelona workshop, Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026 (CEST). All times are local to where the founders are on the day. Every calendar slot is SUGGESTED, not confirmed. Provisional assumptions are marked [ASSUMPTION]. Labels used for outputs: DECISION (agreed, recorded, may carry a review trigger), DRAFT (written but not yet agreed or tested), DEMONSTRATION (the prototype shows a journey works; it proves nothing about demand), VALIDATED FINDING (observed in the world by a founder, with date, count and verbatim quotes).

## 1. Design stance

This design optimises for not fooling ourselves. The package contains twenty-two untested hypotheses and no observed member, recipient, community leader or brand (context_audit.md sections 2 and 4), so the workshop's biggest risk is two people agreeing with each other about things nobody has seen. Every hour that can be spent gathering evidence is moved into the three preparation weekends: a five-person Wizard of Oz week on the Pass (the most consequential assumption), ten community intercepts with a cold recipient-page test, three to five brand-marketer conversations, verbatim public Duel evidence, first-hand use of five alternatives, and an affiliate coverage spreadsheet built from the products real people actually name. The workshop then synthesises that evidence in one session on Saturday morning, makes decisions against it, and locks prototype scope before lunch so Saturday afternoon and Sunday morning are protected build time. What is deliberately sacrificed: breadth (no Collectives, Points or brand-side design in the workshop), polish (the deck is a slide-by-slide argument with evidence gaps, not a designed deck), stretch prototype features (the map and wallet mock only appear if a late flight is booked), and any preparation task that does not produce an observation, a screenshot or a number. The seven days after the workshop are designed as external tests of the decisions, not as internal document tidying.

### D1-02 Evidence synthesis (55 min). Lead Tory. Recorder Blake. Artifact owner Tory (evidence ledger).
- Purpose: what did we actually observe in three weeks, and what does it settle? This is the only session where opinions are not allowed until the evidence is on the table.
- Inputs: P4 WoZ result (moments-used ratio, printed versus on-screen, repeat pairs, verbatims); P5 intercept summary (first tap, emails given out of N, forced ranking, public-versus-private split of My 5 items); P6 brand notes (which event they would pay to see, budget line, signing threshold, CFO number); P7 Duel public evidence; P8 alternatives notes; P9 coverage sheet and £100 arithmetic; P16 statistics sheet.
- Method: (1) 12 min: Tory presents the commercial pack findings, numbers first, verbatims second, no interpretation. (2) 12 min: Blake presents the product pack findings the same way. (3) 20 min: together, fill the evidence ledger: one row per hypothesis from context_audit.md section 2 that the preparation touched (at least 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.7, 2.12, 2.14, 2.16, 2.22), each labelled VALIDATED FINDING (observed, with count and date), HYPOTHESIS (untested or fewer than five observations), or DISPROVED (observed the opposite), with the source note named. (4) 8 min: write the three findings that most change the plan, and the three biggest remaining unknowns. (5) 3 min: recorder reads back; both initial.
- Output: evidence ledger (one sheet) with every row labelled.
- Completion criterion: no row reads "we think"; every VALIDATED FINDING has a count, a date and a founder's name; the three plan-changing findings are written in one sentence each.
- Fallback if late or disagreeing: any row without agreement is labelled HYPOTHESIS and moves on; the ledger is never left unlabelled.

### D1-03 What is Sway (55 min). Lead Blake. Recorder Blake. Artifact owner Tory (one-page thesis).
- Purpose: answer Q1 with a shared, specific, defensible sentence and a one-page thesis that cites the ledger.
- Inputs: evidence ledger; each founder's P2 strategic note (their independent one-sentence description and "what the other may see differently"); context_audit.md 1.3 and 3.8 (the four competing one-liners).
- Method: (1) 8 min silent: each founder writes, without conferring, the category, target customer, founding insight, positioning and reason to exist in five lines, and marks which lines the ledger supports. (2) 7 min: swap and read; each marks the two lines they disagree with. (3) 20 min: converge line by line; where the ledger has a VALIDATED FINDING it governs; where it does not, apply the decision rule (section 12) and record a review trigger. (4) 12 min: Tory drafts the one-page thesis on the wall from the agreed lines, in the fixed order: one sentence, customer, insight, positioning, reason to exist, what we know, what we do not know. (5) 8 min: read aloud, test against the alternatives notes ("could ShopMy, LTK or Duel say this sentence?"; if yes, rewrite), record.
- Output: DECISION: one-sentence company description and Q1 answer; DRAFT: one-page company thesis with a "what we do not know" section.
- Completion criterion: the sentence names a customer and a behaviour, contains no word from the avoid list in context_audit.md section 8, and no incumbent could truthfully say it; every claim on the page has a ledger label.
- Fallback if late or disagreeing: keep the current one thing ("Sway turns trusted product recommendations into measurable commerce") as an uncertain DECISION with review trigger "after the first ten cohort members", and move on at 11:15.

### D1-04 What does it do (55 min). Lead Tory. Recorder Tory. Artifact owner Blake (product definition and priorities).
- Purpose: answer Q2 and choose the one journey the prototype demonstrates, using the WoZ ratio and intercept data rather than preference.
- Inputs: evidence ledger rows 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 2.18; P4 (moments-used ratio, seconds from "can I see that?" to page); P5 (first tap, emails given, public-versus-private split); P8 Tests 3 and 5 (how much of Locker plus Link is a free commodity); prototype_feasibility.md section 9 build brief; the durable core in context_audit.md 1.6.
- Method: (1) 10 min: hero mechanic decision. Read the WoZ ratio aloud. Rule agreed in advance: if the Pass was used in fewer than one in five possible moments, or the median scan-to-page time exceeded 15 seconds, the Link or card is the hero and the Pass is shown second; otherwise the Pass is the hero. Record as DECISION with the number that decided it. (2) 12 min: primary journey written as six numbered steps (advocate side and recipient side) on the wall, in role language ("the advocate", "the recipient"), never founder names. (3) 10 min: what the recipient sees second (product page versus the rest of the advocate's list), earnings shown or hidden by default, recipient identification ask, decided from P5 first-tap and email counts; each a DECISION with review trigger. (4) 10 min: attribution event table: scan, view, save, click, purchase where visible, repeat, downstream recommendation; for each: what is known, what is inferred, who can fake it, what moves Trust, what a brand's finance team could audit in a pilot. (5) 8 min: explicit exclusions and the do-not-build list (start from context_audit.md 1.6 later layers plus discovery); product priorities split into long-term vision, initial MVP, workshop prototype. (6) 5 min: read back, record.
- Output: DECISION: primary journey and hero mechanic; DECISION: attribution event table; DRAFT: product definition (core experience, primary journey, outcome, exclusions); DRAFT: product priorities (vision, MVP, prototype).
- Completion criterion: the journey has six steps or fewer and one dominant recipient action; every exclusion is written; the event table has a "fakeable by" column filled; the prototype column of the priorities matches the build brief exactly.
- Fallback if late or disagreeing: the prototype journey defaults to the build brief in prototype_feasibility.md section 9 (Pass first) as a DEMONSTRATION only, with the hero-mechanic question recorded as an uncertain DECISION to be settled by the first cohort's ratio; the session must end at 12:15 regardless.

## 12. Decision rule for disagreements and how uncertain decisions are recorded

1. Evidence governs. Where the evidence ledger holds a VALIDATED FINDING (observed by a founder, with count, date and verbatim), it decides the point over either founder's preference. Disagreement about what a finding means is timeboxed to 10 minutes by the session lead.
2. Domain owner decides after hearing the objection. Product definition, primary journey, prototype scope and product priorities: Blake decides. Positioning, sales strategy, initial offer, pricing hypotheses and first customers: Tory decides. The deciding founder must first restate the other's objection in one sentence to the other's satisfaction.
3. Consensus items. The one-sentence company description, the Q5 answer, the fundraising narrative and the execution plan need both founders. If there is no consensus after 10 minutes: (a) the position with more ledger support is adopted as an uncertain DECISION; (b) if the ledger supports neither, Tory decides on "what is Sway" and Q5 as originator of the idea, Blake decides on "what does it do"; (c) the dissenting position is recorded verbatim with the evidence that would change the decision.
4. Nothing is a decision without a decision log entry. The fixed entry format is: decision text; label (DECISION, uncertain DECISION, DRAFT, DEMONSTRATION, VALIDATED FINDING); confidence (high, medium, low); evidence cited (ledger row or source note); dissent (verbatim, or "none"); review trigger; review date; owner who calls the review.
5. Review triggers must be observable events or numbers, never feelings. Examples the workshop should use: "the Pass is used in fewer than 1 in 5 possible moments in the first cohort's first two weeks"; "fewer than 3 of 10 cold-test recipients tap the dominant action first"; "fewer than 40 per cent of products in the first fifty Lockers are wellness-brand products"; "the first brand marketer to hear the offer names a different budget line"; "publisher approval from Awin or impact.com has not arrived by Fri 13 Nov". Review dates fall no later than the 90-day plan's end and are checked at the Friday weekly review.
6. Labels are policed at D2-05. The prototype is always a DEMONSTRATION. Nothing becomes a VALIDATED FINDING because the demo worked, because both founders agree, or because a research file inferred it. The deck, the sales strategy and the product definition remain DRAFT wherever their claims rest on a HYPOTHESIS, and say so in the document.
7. Blake abstains on any claim about Duel beyond public pages, and the abstention is recorded each time it applies.
