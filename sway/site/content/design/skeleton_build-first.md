---

## 1. Design stance

This skeleton optimises for two things: a prototype that demonstrably works on two phones by Saturday afternoon, and a hard link between what is built and what is sold, so that no sentence in the sales strategy or the deck claims something the prototype cannot show. To get there, the five strategy questions are compressed into five 30-minute decision gates that run on positions each founder has written in preparation (nothing is argued from a blank page), scope is locked at 11:30 on Saturday, and Blake then gets three uninterrupted build blocks on Saturday (4 hours 00 minutes) and two on Sunday (1 hour 45 minutes), with Tory running the commercial deliverables in parallel and testing at seven fixed checkpoints. What is deliberately sacrificed: breadth of strategic exploration (the recommended "industry shifts" and "mission and values" conversations in `05_FOUNDER_WORKING_SESSION.md` are not scheduled; they are absorbed into the position papers and the one-page thesis); the pilot-design outputs that need evidence the founders do not yet have (city, node list, 30/60/90-day thresholds are recorded as open decisions with owners and dates in the execution plan rather than decided in the room); and polish on the prototype (map, wallet card, add-product sheet are cut first). The prototype is a demonstration and a stimulus, never evidence of demand, and the programme says so on every deliverable.

## 12. Decision rule for disagreements, and how uncertain decisions are recorded

Rule 1, the timebox is the arbiter. Every gate has a 12-minute discussion and a 7-minute writing step. When the writing step starts, the lead calls the decision. Nothing is reopened after its gate except through a review trigger.

Rule 2, domain owner decides provisionally. Product definition, prototype scope, product priorities and the do-not-build list: Blake decides. Positioning, ICP, offer, pricing hypotheses, first customers and the deck's argument: Tory decides. The five answers and the company thesis are joint: if the two founders cannot agree inside the timebox, the answer is written in the domain owner's version (questions 2 and 5 product-side, Blake; questions 1, 3 and 4 commercial-side, Tory) and labelled Provisional with the other founder's dissent in one sentence and their "what would change my mind" line from the position paper copied in.

Rule 3, the prototype wins in the room, the evidence wins afterwards. If a sales or deck claim needs something the prototype does not show, the claim is downgraded to Hypothesis, never the other way round; the scope lock is not reopened to rescue a claim. After the workshop, the review trigger is an evidence event, and the founder who was overruled owns collecting it.

Rule 4, no decisions by exhaustion. No strategic decision is made after 18:00 on Saturday or in the contingency blocks; anything still open goes to the log as Provisional with a date.

Rule 5, Duel. Any statement about Duel that is not on a public page is out of order; Tory leads Gate 3 and the differentiation thesis, and Blake's abstention is recorded in the log.

Recording. The decision log has one row per decision with: ID; text (one sentence); type (Decision, Draft, Demonstration, Validated finding); confidence (Firm or Provisional); evidence basis (labels and file names); dissent (who, one sentence); review trigger (the observable event that reopens it, a date, an owner); consequence if reversed (what changes in the product, the sales strategy or the deck). A Provisional decision without a review trigger is not allowed to leave the room. Example trigger forms: "reopen if fewer than 3 of 10 intercept respondents would show a QR to a friend, by Fri 23 Oct, owner Tory"; "reopen if fewer than 40 per cent of products in the first 20 Lockers are wellness-brand products, by Fri 20 Nov, owner Blake". Validated findings can only come from a dated conversation, test or observation recorded in an evidence file; the prototype never produces one.
